# Section 28: Maternity & Pregnancy


## 28.1 Purpose of the Policy

28.1.1  The purpose of this Policy is to communicate the statutory requirement, so that Employees may understand and
benefit from the protection afforded them during pregnancy and after returning from maternity.
28.1.2  The Policy outlines the obligations on the Employer and Employee during the period of pregnancy and

maternity leave.
28.1.3  The Policy details the support that is available during the period of pregnancy and maternity and encourages open
communication which will help both parties and ensure any problems or difficulties can be resolved as quickly

as possible.

## 28.2 Purpose and Scope

28.2.1  The Company will not treat pregnant workers any less favourably as a result of their condition or their intention to
exercise the right to maternity provisions.
28.2.2  The Company recognises it has a duty of care to the Employee during the pregnancy and maternity period. Employees
should recognise they are still under contract and have obligations relating to professionalism and confidentiality.
28.2.3  The Company strongly believes in, and supports open transparent communication during this period.

## 28.3 Definitions Used in the Pregnancy and Maternity Policy and Procedure

28.3.1  EWC:  The week, beginning on the Sunday, that is the expected date of confinement.
28.3.2  Qualifying Week: The 15th week before the expected week of confinement, used for the calculation of Maternity Pay.
28.3.3  SMP:  Statutory Maternity Pay, the weekly allowance, set by the Government which, if eligible, is paid by the Company.
28.3.4  SMA: Statutory Maternity Allowance, the weekly allowance, set by the Government and paid by the Government in
cases where Employees are not entitled to SMP.
28.3.5  OML:  Ordinary Maternity Leave, the period of maternity leave from 1 – 28 weeks.
28.3.6  AML:  Additional Maternity Leave, the period of maternity leave from 28 – 52 weeks

## 28.4 Informing Us You are Pregnant

28.4.1  The Company understands the excitement and apprehension Employees may feel during the early stages of pregnancy
and would encourage early notification of your pregnancy, so that full support can be given to the Employee.  Discretion
will be used, as the Company recognises the Employee may wish to keep their pregnancy private for a period of time.
28.4.2  Employees must inform the Company by the 15th week prior to the EDOC.  In most cases, this is the point that the
Midwife Team will provide Employees with the MAT B1 Form.  This is a form which confirms the pregnancy and
expected date of confinement.

**28.4.3**
Employees are entitled to reasonable paid time off work to attend antenatal and child craft appointments as advised
by their Doctor, Registered Midwife, or registered Health Worker. Where possible, the Company may request the
Employee to attend such appointments outside the standard works hours or at least at one end of the day. Except in
the case of the first appointment, Employees may be requested to produce evidence of the appointment, such as a
Medical Certificate or Appointment Card.

**28.4.4**
Employees should endeavour to give their Line Manager as much notice as possible of the appointments, and where
possible book the appointments for the start or the end of the day.
28.4.5   Employees who are partners/fathers may also have time off to attend two ante natal appointments up to a maximum
of 6.5 hours per appointment.  This time will be unpaid and available to all staff whatever their length of service.


## 28.5 Risk Assessments

28.5.1  Under the Management of Health and Safety at Work Regulations 1999, the Company has a duty to take care of the
Health and Safety of all Employees.  This includes undertaking Risk Assessments for those who are pregnant, have
given birth within the last six months, or are breast feeding.
28.5.2  The Risk Assessment will be undertaken by an appropriately trained Line Manager or Facilities Manager, unless
Employees request an alternative, such as a member of the Human Resources Department.
28.5.3  The Risk Assessment will be reviewed at regular intervals to take into account the changing physique and impact this
may have on the Employee’s ability to safely complete their contractual role but at all times the Employee can raise
any issues or concerns and does not need to wait for a formal Risk Assessment.
28.5.4  The Company will endeavour, where practicable, to introduce a safe system of work to minimise risk to the Employee
to an acceptable level. Where it is not reasonably practicable to eliminate sufficient risk from the Employee’s role, and
no suitable alternative role can be found, an Employee may be suspended until such time as a satisfactory resolution
can be found. Employees so suspended will receive normal basic pay for this period, but not overtime, commission or
discretionary bonuses.
28.5.5  Employment will continue during the period of the suspension and it does not in any way affect the Employee’s
entitlement to Statutory Maternity rights.

## 28.6 Entitlement to Maternity Leave

28.6.1  The current entitlement of Maternity Leave is up to 52 weeks Maternity Leave.  This is regardless of age, length of
service, or the number of occasions Maternity Leave has been taken previously.
28.6.2  Maternity Leave is made up of 28 weeks OML and 28 weeks AML.  It can start at any point from 11 weeks prior to the
birth, unless the child is born prematurely, in which case it will start the day before the baby is born.

**28.6.3**
Mothers will continue to be entitled to 52 weeks of Maternity Leave and 39 weeks of Statutory Maternity Pay or
Maternity Allowance.  If they choose to, an eligible mother can end her maternity leave early and, with her partner,
or the child’s father, opt for [Shared Parental Leave](38-shared-parental-leave.md) instead of Maternity Leave. (See [Section 34](34-paternity.md) [Shared Parental Leave](38-shared-parental-leave.md)
Policy and Procedure).
28.6.4   [Paternity Leave](34-paternity.md) will continue to be available to those partners/fathers who do not opt for [Shared Parental Leave](38-shared-parental-leave.md) (See
[Section 33](33-parental-bereavement.md) [Paternity Leave](34-paternity.md) and Policy).

## 28.7 Eligibility to Maternity Pay

28.7.1  The Employee must have:
- demonstrated they have been employed continuously for 28 weeks into the 15th week before the baby is due
(qualifying week);
- have average earnings in the eight weeks up to and including the qualifying week that are not less than the
lower earnings limit for National Insurance Contributions;
- is still pregnant 11 weeks before the start of the expected week of child birth;
- a MAT B1 Form provided by a registered Midwife or healthcare professional;
- provided appropriate notification to the Company.
28.7.2  The amount of SMP provided is set by the Government each year. Payments are subject to Tax and National Insurance
deductions which will vary depending on individual’s circumstances.
28.7.3  SMP will be paid regardless of whether the Employee returns to work or not.
28.7.4  The first six weeks are calculated at 90% of average earnings which includes overtime, bonus and any one off payments
other than discretionary bonuses.  Calculation for this period is eight weeks prior to the expected date of confinement,
however, where there has been any pay rise the Company will recalculate the payment.

## 28.9 Method of payment

28.9.1  In the event of any pay rise, after the calculation period, but prior to the end of the 52-week maternity period, the
maternity pay will be recalculated and adjusted. In some cases, this may result in a back payment.
28.9.2  If the Employee is not entitled to SMP, a form will be provided to establish if they are eligible for Statutory Maternity
Allowance. It is the Employee’s responsibility to complete and return this documentation.
28.10. 	 Benefits during Maternity Leave
28.10.1  Throughout Maternity Leave, the Employee will continue to benefit from all the terms and conditions of your
employment except those relating to salary or wages.
28.10.2  During the period of maternity, the Employee’s salary will be replaced with SMP where eligible.
28.10.3  Employees are encouraged to take any outstanding Annual Leave due to them before the commencement of Ordinary
Maternity Leave. If this is not possible outstanding annual leave will be paid as a one-off payment.
28.10.4  Employee’s/Employer’s Pension contributions will continue to be made during the period when the Employee is
receiving SMP but not during any period of unpaid Additional Maternity Leave.  Please note Employer’s pension
contributions will only be made if the matching Employee’s pension contributions are made. These contributions
will be as a percentage of the Company maternity pay of SM, whichever is relevant. The Company contributions will
continue to be based on the Employees basic salary, rather than on the maternity pay.
28.10.5  Annual Leave will continue to be accrued during the Maternity Leave Period, whether OML or AML.
28.10.6  In the year in which maternity leave commences or ceases, the Employees total holiday entitlement will be their
normal company entitlement, plus the number of bank holiday declared for the year. Any holiday taken and any bank
holidays occurring during the period of working, rather than maternity leave will be deducted from the total.
28.10.7  Any holiday untaken at the end of the relevant holiday year will be paid in the January of the following year.
28.10.8  Sales bonuses will be included within the salary upon which the first six weeks’ maternity pay is calculated, but will not
be payable thereafter.

## 28.11 Illness During Pregnancy

28.11.1  Employees do not generally choose to be unwell during pregnancy, however minor problems such as morning sickness,
heat intolerance and reduced energy levels may affect the Employees ability to consistently perform at 100%.
28.11.2  The Company encourages Employees to be pro-active in their management of illness, for example starting later if
suffering from extreme morning sickness.
28.11.3  The Company understands maternity can place the body under additional pressure, emotional stress and the
development of conditions, which would normally not occur.
28.11.4  Absence may be discussed as part of the routine Risk Assessments, which may result in utilising Annual Leave to
reduce periods in work, alternating working hours to accommodate later starts etc. Any changes will be by mutual
agreement and temporary for the duration of the pregnancy.
28.11.5  Treatment will be no less favourable for pregnancy related illnesses. Management of, payment and entitlements to
Company Sick Pay will be in line with standard Company procedures.
28.11.6 If absence occurs within four weeks of the EDOC, whether wholly or partly because of the pregnancy, the Employer
will be required to place the Employee on Maternity Leave. This will be effective from the first day following the first
day of absence.
28.12. 	 Notifying Us of your Wish to Start Maternity Leave
28.12.1  The Company requires formal, written notification of an Employee’s intention to start Maternity Leave, on the form
set out at the end of this Policy. (Appendix 28.1).
28.12.2  Once the Company receives notification of the Maternity Leave, the company will send a written response confirming
the intention and detailing the amount of Maternity Leave Employees are entitled to. (Appendix 28.2).
28.12.3  The Company will endeavour to take a flexible approach, however absence within four weeks of the EDOC will
automatically trigger the start of the Maternity Leave.
28.12.4  Maternity Leave can start on the following:
- The Employee’s chosen start date, but no earlier than 11 weeks before the EDOC;
- The day after the Employee gives birth;
- The day after any day on which Employee is absent for a pregnancy related reason, in the four weeks before
the EDOC.
28.13. 	 Still Births and Miscarriages
28.13.1  In the sad event an Employee’s child is Still Born 24 weeks or more into the pregnancy they will retain their entitlement
to Maternity Leave and Statutory Maternity Pay.
28.13.2  A miscarriage prior to 24 weeks will mean the Employee is not entitled to Maternity Leave and Statutory Maternity
Pay, but will be entitled to Company and Statutory Sick Pay in the normal way.
28.13.3  Where a child is born, and lives for a short time, then subject to current Government definition for the qualifying time,
the Employee will retain their full rights to Maternity Leave and Statutory Maternity Pay, irrespective of the timings of
the birth.
28.14. 	 Whilst on Maternity Leave
28.14.1  The Company recognises up to one year away from work is a significant period of time, therefore, the Company will
endeavour to maintain a reasonable level of contact with Employees.
28.14.2  In April 2007 the Government introduced ‘Keeping in Touch Days’, also known as KITs. The Company can request
that you attend the work place for up to ten days during your Maternity Leave, without your attendance having any
detrimental impact on your Maternity Pay.
28.14.3  This attendance could be for the purpose of training, staff meetings, updates and handovers.


28.14.4  The Company may, at its option either pay the Employee for KITs worked or opt to operate a ‘time in lieu’ policy for the
KITs. In the event of the Company opting to operate a ‘time in lieu’ policy, Employees will be able to use their accrued
time in lieu once they return to work. This may help the Employee during the first few weeks back at work and ease the
transition from Maternity Leave to work for both Mother and Baby. If the Company elects to pay KITs, the Employee
will be paid for these in the month they are worked.
28.14.5 During the remainder of an Employee’s period of Maternity Leave, the Company may contact the Employee about
the possibility of working on KITs. The Employee may also write to the Company to request to work on KITs However,
the Employee is under no obligation to agree to attend work and the Company is under no obligation to offer the
Employee KITs.  It is entirely the Employee’s decision and the Employee will not be subjected to any form of detriment
if they do not wish to agree.
28.14.6  Employees will lose one week’s SMP for any week in which they agree to work for the Company over and above the
10 KITs.
28.14.7  The Company will endeavour to keep Employees updated on any changes in structure, appointments or vacancies in
their department.
28.14.8  The Company will ensure all Employees on Maternity Leave are invited to all official Company functions.
28.15. 	 Notice to end Maternity Leave/[Shared Parental Leave](38-shared-parental-leave.md)
28.15.1   The mother must give the Company eight weeks notice to end her entitlement to Maternity Leave early.  This notice
can be given before or after the birth.  If notice is given after the birth this notice is binding, however, if notice is given
before the birth, the mother is able to revoke the notice up to 6 weeks after the birth.
28.16  	 Returning From Maternity Leave
28.16.1  While the Employee is under no obligation to do so, it would assist the Company if they confirm as soon as convenient
during their Maternity Leave that they will be returning to work as expected.
28.16.2  If the Employee decides not to return to work after Maternity, they must give 8 weeks notice of resignation.
28.16.3  The Company accepts Employee’s legal rights to return to their contractual role on the same Terms and Conditions
as if they had not been absent. If this return is not reasonably practical i.e. the role no longer exists, the Employee
is entitled to return to a suitable role on terms which are similar to those prior to the Maternity Leave or opt for
Redundancy.
28.16.4  The Employee has no automatic right to return to work on a part time basis, or make changes to their working pattern.
Should Employees wish to make changes to their working pattern or role, they will be required to submit a formal
request, as detailed in the [Flexible Working Policy and Procedure](36-flexible-working.md).
28.16.5  The Employee must provide eight weeks notice if a change in return date is required. The Company reserves the right
to postpone the return to work date if the appropriate notice is not provided.
28.16.6  The Employee is prohibited from returning for a two-week period, or four weeks if in a Production role, following

child birth.
28.16.7  The Company participates in a Child Care Voucher Scheme; more information is available from Human Resources or
Payroll.  This scheme will only be continued for as long as it is government sponsored and supported. (Appendix 28.3).
28.17. 	 Grievances
28.17.1  If the Employee to which the policy applies feels they have been treated unfairly by an individual, or their Line Manager,
they should refer to the [Grievance Procedure](21-grievance.md) of the Handbook.

will alter automatically when, and if, the statutory provisions alter. The provisions of this policy do not form part of
your Employment Contract and do not contractually bind the Company.
