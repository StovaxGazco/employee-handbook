## 26.1 Purpose and Scope


**26.1.1** The Company recognises that it has a civic responsibility to allow Employees to take on public and civic duties, attend jury service or to be part of the volunteer reserve forces. (“Public Duties”).

**26.1.2** This Policy is not intended to restrict or discourage involvement in such duties but is designed to ensure all Employees are aware of the Company procedure to be followed.

**26.1.3** This policy applies to all permanent Employees of the Company, irrespective of seniority or department.

## 26.2 Policy Principles


**26.2.1** In some cases, people carrying out “Public Duties” may encounter difficulties in balancing the demands of those duties with the needs of their job. Human Resources will be able to offer advice on the operation of these guidelines, as they apply in particular cases.

**26.2.2** Leave for “Public Duties” other than Jury Service, will be unpaid in most cases.

**26.3.1** Jury Service and Attendance at Court

**26.3.1.1** Leave for Jury Service and attendance at Court shall be granted in accordance with the relevant statutory requirements. If an Employee is required to attend court for jury service or as a witness, they will receive notification from the local court detailing the dates they are required to attend. Included in this notification will be a Jurors Loss of Earnings Certificate, which Employees should complete to claim loss of earnings, travel and subsistence expenses from the Clerk of the Court. However, where, in the Company’s or the Employees view, the release of an Employee for jury service will cause major staffing or operational problems, assistance will be given to the Employee in order to appeal to the Court to re-arrange or cancel the dates of service. (Note, this can usually only be done once).

**26.3.1.3** Employees should provide a copy of the court citation, which is to be retained in the Employees personnel file. Employees are reminded to advise Human Resources on their return to work from court attendance.

**26.3.1.4** The Company will pay the difference to make up full pay for the first 10 days of Jury Service.   However, where the Employee is not required to be in attendance, for any reason, including adjournment, they will be expected to return to work to carry out their normal duties.

**26.3.2** Civic and Public Duties

**26.3.2.1** There are a number of essential “Public Duties” which Employees may wish to undertake or that are required by legislation. Employees have a legal right to reasonable time off to attend to any “Public Duties”. This legal right applies to Employees who are for example:
- A Magistrate, sometimes known as a Justice of the Peace;
- A local councillor;
- A school governor;


- A member of a policy authority;
- A member of any statutory tribunal (e.g. an Employment Tribunal);
- A member of the managing or governing body of an educational establishment;
- A member of the General Teaching Councils for England and Wales.

**26.3.2.2** If you qualify, you are allowed reasonable time off to go to meetings or to carry out your duties. The time must be agreed with your Line Manager beforehand, your Line Manager may refuse your request if it is unreasonable.

**26.3.2.3** If qualifying, Employees, who fall into the above categories must negotiate with their Line Manager (in conjunction with Human Resources) on how much leave may be taken.

**26.3.2.4** Whether an Employees request is ‘reasonable’ will depend on:
- What the duties are;
- What time needed to carry them out;
- The impact on the business, current circumstance and the effect absence may have upon it;
- How much time off has been previously allowed for public duties.

## 26.4 Requesting Leave for Public and Civic Duties


**26.4.1** When requesting time off, Employees should give as much notice as possible, indicating the reasons for taking the time off, and the amount of time that they expect to be required.

**26.4.2** Employees are required to submit completed Requests for Public and Civil Duties Leave form for authorisation to their Line Manager as early as possible, normally giving a minimum of two weeks’ notice.

**26.4.3** Line Managers will endeavour to respond to Requests for Leave for Public and Civic Duties within a maximum of 5 working days and confirm to the Employee whether the request has been granted or declined.

**26.4.4** Any supporting documentation (e.g. the Court Citation) must be attached to the request form, failure to attach the supporting documentation will render the application invalid. The form should be authorised or rejected by the Line Manager and forwarded to Human Resources.
