# Section 24: IT Systems Use

24.

### It Systems Use Policy And Procedure

24.1.
Purpose and Scope

**24.1.1** This Policy and Procedure is designed to protect the Company and its Employees from the risk of damage to, loss or

misuse of its intellectual property.

**24.1.2** The use of authorised IT systems within the Company is required to ensure efficient, accurate and secure business

operations can be carried out.  Inappropriate use of these systems, however, causes many problems.  This Policy sets
out the correct use of the systems and explains the procedures Employees are required to follow.
24.1.3
The Company will apply these policies in conjunction with NIBE IT Policies
24.2.
General Systems Procedure

**24.2.1** Physical Access to Computer Equipment:  No computer equipment will be removed from site without the authority of

the Group IT Director or Managing Director, with the exception of equipment issued to field based or Working From
Home Employees, for the purpose of their job roles.  Failure to comply may be treated as a disciplinary offence. All
equipment is to be signed out on the Asset Register and must be returned on or before the date agreed and booked
back in, as per the attached form (Appendix 23.1).  Any damage or malfunction of such equipment is to be reported
to the IT department by the following day.
24.2.2
Portable computer equipment (laptops, tablets, mobile phones etc.) are to be kept secure when off site and must not
be left unattended in public places, on customer or supplier sites or in parked cars.  Computer equipment is not to be
loaned to or used by anyone other than an Employee without the express permission of the Group Director of IT or a
Company Director.
24.2.3
Passwords: All Employees must keep their passwords confidential.  They must not be disclosed to any other Employee
or third party.  Any passwords suspected of being compromised must be changed immediately or reported to the IT
Department so they can take the necessary actions to prevent unauthorised access. No attempt should be made to
access any system / database / network folders etc. that the user is not authorised to or trained in the use of. Failure
to comply with this may result in the Employee being subject to disciplinary action.

**24.2.4** Phishing attempts have become a serious threat to the Company’s data and financial resources. Phishing is a form

of scam where bad actors will pretend to be someone familiar, usually a manager, customer or supplier, and attempt
to acquire user login information, gain control of IT equipment or extract financial payments. All of the Company’s
Directors and Senior Management Team are happy for you to verbally check or question them on any electronic
request you receive purporting to be from them that requires you to engage in a financial transaction that appears
unusual, urgent and / or not previously discussed. Do not reply to any suspicious email. If you have to send an email,
create a new one using your Company email account and add the recipient to the email, so you know you are emailing
the real mail account. Personal social media and email accounts should never be used to conduct Company business.
The use of Company equipment for personal purposes does not guarantee your personal activities or transactions are
safeguarded.

Be aware of the following during all forms of communication.
- Check the ‘from’ address – The sender’s name may look familiar, but the associated email address may
be unusual
- Is the greeting impersonal or inappropriate? Is there poor spelling, grammar and presentation? – Scammers
are getting better at this but there is often a clue in the way they address you and the format of the email.
- Check contact information and other weblinks – hover your mouse over any weblinks to see where the link
will take you if it was clicked on. Contact information on these emails may also be incorrect.
- Check branding – Real companies spend a lot of time and money in this area and there are often subtle
differences in the official branding from legitimate senders compared to the phishing version.
- Check if the linked website is legitimate – do not click on links that ask you to check an urgent message on
your account. Go to the official site via a web browser and log in that way. If you have clicked on a weblink
then check the web address of the site. Hover your mouse over any links on the site and the link address will
be displayed in the bottom left-hand corner of your browser.
- Asking for personal, security or financial details? – Official and reputable company emails will never ask for
personal or security details via email. Google their phone number and call them if unsure.
- Trying hard to be ‘official’? – the use of supposed ID or account numbers and messages shouting ‘official!’ is
a common trick. Check your own records to verify any details.

- Trying to rush you? – exclusive and time-sensitive deals are designed for you to drop your guard. It’s better to
miss out on a genuine deal than risk compromising your personal details or money.
- Social Media – reading articles and searching for information declares your interest in something. Don’t be
surprised if you start getting targeted ads and emails. In fact, tread more carefully.

Phishing is also prevalent via text messages, social media and telephone so always be aware that something may not
be what it seems and report all instances of phishing attempts.
24.2.5
WhatsApp

WhatsApp is a commonly used app in advanced Social Engineering attacks. This app is currently actively being used
against Group employees. Please note:
- No corporate communication is to be carried out by Stovax Gazco employees using WhatsApp. This is a NIBE
Group policy. Exemptions may be made if WhatsApp is required for external communication within Sales or
with suppliers. This should only be done with the knowledge of the employees’ line Director and all such
correspondence should be assessed for its validity and risk,
- If any communication claiming to be from Stovax Gazco or NIBE is received via WhatsApp, please notify the
Company IT Department immediately. Even if the message claims to be urgent or secretive, always notify the
Company IT Department as this a common method of fraud.
- New technologies such as deep fakes (AI generated pictures and voices) are being used to specifically target
people in leadership roles due to their increased digital footprint. All employees should be aware of these
threats in both business and personal electronic communication.
- Only approved messaging applications are to be used.
24.2.6
Artificial Intelligence (AI)

Purpose:

The purpose of this policy is to establish guidelines for the appropriate, secure and responsible use of AI tools by our
employees. The policy aims to ensure that the use of AI tools is consistent with the Company’s values, core business
objectives and security policies.

Scope:

This policy applies to all any employees who use or intend to use AI tools in the course of their official duties.

Policy:
- Purpose of AI Tools: Employees are permitted to use AI tools only for approved business purposes such as,
but not limited to, research, report writing, systems development, improving workflow, product design etc.
The use of AI tools for personal use, non-approved business use or any other purpose is strictly prohibited.
Employees are to get prior authorisation from their department Director before using AI tools for any business
task or project.
- Data Protection and Privacy: Employees are required to ensure that data is protected at all times when using
AI tools. This includes safeguarding commercially sensitive information and complying with Company policies
related to data privacy. No person-identifiable data must be entered into any AI tool. GDPR compliance must
be adhered to at all times.
- Security: Employees must ensure that AI tools are used in a secure manner, in accordance with Company
policies and industry standards. This includes ensuring that AI tools are not used to transmit viruses or
malware or to engage in any other unauthorized or malicious activity. Only company AI user accounts are to
be used. Under no circumstances are personal user accounts for AI tools to be used on Company equipment.
- Accuracy: Employees must ensure that AI tools are used in a manner that ensures the accuracy and reliability
of the results produced by these tools. This includes verifying the accuracy of the input data and the algorithms
used by the AI tools. Plagiarism and the breach of UK or international copyright and intellectual property laws
must be avoided when using AI tools for any purpose where the information generated by an AI tool is used
for commercial purposes or for public use. Where possible, work must be identified as being completed with
the use of AI tools.
- Professionalism: All employees must ensure that the use of AI tools is consistent with the company’s values
and standards of professionalism. This includes using AI tools in a respectful and courteous manner, avoiding
offensive language or behaviour, and ensuring that the results produced by AI tools are appropriate for the
intended audience, systems and purpose.
- Monitoring: The use of AI tools by employees may be monitored by the Company to ensure compliance with
this policy and applicable laws and regulations. Monitoring may include reviewing chat logs, firewall traffic,
analysing output data, and conducting periodic audits of AI tool usage throughout the business.

- Incident Reporting: Employees must report any security or data breaches and all other incidents related to
the use of AI tools to their supervisor and the Company’s IT department immediately.
- Specific Usage: Due to the vast number of potential uses of AI technology, more specific departmental sub-
policies or work instructions will be created by departmental heads to ensure the appropriate scope for using
AI tools is known for each business function. Any formal use of data or information resulting from the use of AI
tools is to be documented and approved by the employees’ departmental Director. All use of AI output must
be thoroughly vetted and tested before being applied within the business. Under no circumstances should AI
tools be used to alter the functionality, data or programming of any Company or 3rd party systems without
approval from the IT Department.

Enforcement:

Violation of this policy may result in disciplinary action, up to and including the termination of employment. The
Company reserves the right to take legal action against individuals who violate this policy or engage in illegal, malicious
or commercially damaging activity using AI tools.
24.2.7
Authorised Maintenance:  Maintenance to computer equipment is only to be carried out by members of the IT
Department or their approved agents.  The re-configuration of Company computer equipment by non-Company_IT
personnel is strictly forbidden.
24.2.8
Remote Network:  Remote connection to the Company’s network is only to be done using approved VPN tools.  VPN
connections are to be switched off when not in use.  IP addresses for VPN connections are not to be disclosed to
anyone outside of the Company without the prior written permission of the Group Director of IT or the Managing
Director.  Remote network connections must use the approved multi-factor authentication (MFA) methods advised
by the IT Department. Only MFA requests initiated by the employee themselves should be accepted. If an employee
is prompted to accept an MFA request they did not initiate it should be rejected and the incident reported to the
Company IT Department immediately.
24.2.9
Rules Concerning the Control of Mobile Computing and Storage Devices:  With continued advances in computer
technology, mobile computing and storage devices have become useful tools to meet business needs.  The Company
may issue any type of mobile device to allow Employees to carry out their duties.  These devices are especially
susceptible to loss, theft, hacking, and the distribution of malicious software because they are easily portable and can
be used discretely anywhere.  Mobile devices must be password protected and encrypted where possible.  Mobile
computing and storage devices include, but are not limited to:-
- Laptop Computers;
- Personal Digital Assistants (PDA’s), mobile Phones and Tablets;
- Universal Serial Bus Port Devices (USB);
- Compact Discs (CDs)/Digital Versatile Discs (DVDs);
- Flash/Pen/Memory Drives;
- External Hard Disk Drives;
- Handheld Wireless Scanners;
- Any other existing or future mobile computing or storage device.

**24.2.10** No Employee may download any data or Company information onto a mobile computing or storage device without

their Line Manager’s prior approval.   All such data must be kept secure and taken off Company premises for the
minimum time necessary.  All data must then be returned to the Company premises and removed from the mobile
computing or storage device.

**24.2.11** No Employee must bring any unauthorised mobile computing or storage device into the Company premises with the

intention of connecting them (wired, WI-FI, Bluetooth, NFC etc.) to the Company’s network and/or downloading any
Company data without the prior written permission of the Group’s IT Director or Managing Director.
24.3
Email System and Internet Access
24.3.1
Authorised Use:  The Email system and Internet access are made available for communication on matters directly
concerned with the business of the Company.  Employees using the Email system should give particular attention to
the following points:

**24.3.2** The Standard of Presentation:  The style and content of an email message must be consistent with the standards that

the Company expects from written communications. All email messages must use the latest ‘House’ style as provided
by the Marketing Department.

24.3.3
The Extent of Circulation:  Email messages should only be sent to those internal or external recipients for whom they
are particularly relevant.
24.3.4
The Appropriateness of Email: Email should not be used as a substitute for face-to-face communication.  Emails should
not infringe Equal Opportunities, Health and Safety or Anti-Discrimination Policies.  Emails must not contain defamatory
or libellous statements.  Legal and contractual implications on all correspondence should also be considered and
advice taken if unsure.
24.3.5
The Visibility of Email:  If the message is confidential, the user must ensure that the necessary steps are taken to
protect confidentiality and that it is only sent to the correct people.
24.3.6
Email Contracts:  Offers or contracts transmitted via email are as legally binding on the Company as those sent on
paper.  Business language must be used at all times and all external communications must be written in the form of
a letter, not in abbreviated form.  The Company’s current ‘liability and disclaimer signature’ must be attached to all
external email.  This may be applied automatically by the Company’s email servers as the email is sent. Employees who
are unsure about this should seek appropriate training.
24.3.7
Virus Checking:  All incoming attachments from external sources must be virus checked prior to opening.  This may
be done either by the individual or by the IT department.  This check will normally be executed automatically by the
Group’s IT systems.  If however, an error message or notification is received by the Employee, then they should contact
the IT department for guidance and ensure that the attachment is not forwarded internally until it has been further
checked.
24.3.8
Frivolous Use:  System resources are not unlimited.  Network and Internet bandwidth and storage capacity have finite
limits, and all users connected to the network have a responsibility to conserve these resources.  As such, the user
must not deliberately perform acts that waste computer resources or unfairly monopolise resources to the exclusion
of others.  These acts include, but are not limited to, sending mass mailings or chain letters, spending excessive
amounts of time on the internet, playing games, engaging in online chat groups and personal social media accounts,
uploading or downloading large files, accessing unauthorised streaming audio or video content, or otherwise creating
unnecessary loads on network infrastructure associated with non-business related uses of the internet.
24.4
Unauthorised Use

**24.4.1** Employees must only use IT systems that are required to fulfil their duties and must not use such systems to do the

following:-
- Create any message that could constitute bullying or harassment (e.g. on the grounds of sex, race or disability);
- Inappropriate personal use e.g. excessive or offensive jokes, social media use, audio or video distribution etc;
- On-line gambling, drugs, pornography;
- Download or distribute copyright information and/or any software available to the user without first checking
with the Group Director of IT to see if that is appropriate;
- Distributing confidential information about other Employees, the Company, its business activities or its
customers or suppliers;
- Installing new or unauthorised software.  If unsure, the Employee must check with the IT department before
installing;
- Download files from the internet that are for use outside of their job role or without the permission of IT. This
includes shareware, freeware, computer games or anything that breaches UK or international intellectual
property or copyright laws;
- On-line dating;
- Personal social media websites or applications, including, but not exhaustive to Facebook, YouTube, Twitter,
Instagram, Snapchat, WhatsApp (see above) or any other such similar services’
- Use authorised file sharing (e.g. bit torrent) or file transfer services, including FTP and online file transfer sites;
- No attempt is to be made to access or install software capable of accessing the Dark Web;
- Job searching outside of the Company;
- Soliciting money for religious, political and any other unauthorised causes.
24.4.2
In the event that an individual accesses an unauthorised / illegal / offensive site in error e.g. whilst searching for a
legitimate site, that individual should notify Human Resources, the IT Department or their Line Manager immediately.
24.4.3
Personal use of the web and email systems must only be at break time, or outside standard working hours, including
responding to personal emails, unless they are of a critical nature.

24.4.4
Employees accessing the Internet do so at their own risk. The Company cannot accept responsibility for material
viewed or downloaded or personal data stolen or copied whilst accessing the web through the Company’s

computer systems.
24.4.5
Exceptional authorisation to access social media sites and job sites may be given by a Director or Human Resources to
facilitate company recruitment or for any other reason they consider appropriate.
24.5
Implementation of the Policy
24.5.1
The Company will appoint a nominated individual to be responsible for the management of the system.  This person
will be available for advice on all aspects of the Policy.  The nominated individual will be the Human Resources Director,
or if unavailable, the Group IT Director.

**24.5.2** Training will be given to familiarise new Employees with the Company’s IT systems.  Managers are required to ensure

that all new Employees are properly trained prior to using any of these systems.  Online IT Awareness training must be
completed immediately upon request.
24.5.3
Regular monitoring of email messages will be carried out on a random basis.  This will be carried out by the IT
department.  Inappropriate use, or breach of the Policy, may result in disciplinary proceedings.
24.5.4
All Employees are advised that the Company can and will monitor Internet and E-mail usage for inappropriate
behaviour and may access and disclose data stored on the Stovax Group’s computers, regardless of whether or not
the use is business related or personal.  Employees should be aware that security systems used by the Company are
capable of recording (for each and every user) each internet site visit, each chat, newsgroup or email message and
each file transfer into and out of our internal networks.  The Company reserves the right to review this data at any
time.  No Employee will have any right to privacy or confidentiality as to their internet usage.
24.5.5
Employee’s attention is drawn to the requirements of the Data Protection Act 2018 (GDPR).  This Act covers data
stored and processed in the UK.  All Employees are required to comply with the requirements of the Act regardless
of the location of the user at the point of access.  Further information is available from the Group IT Director, Human
Resources or the Government website, https://ico.org.uk/.
24.5.6
All system users have their own unique individual passwords that will require changing periodically and must remain
confidential to the user.  It is forbidden to use any account or password other than the one issued to you.  It is also
forbidden to knowingly allow another person to log on using your account and password without the permission of
the Group IT Director or Managing Director.  Access to any system using another Employee’s credentials, without prior
authorisation is likely to result in disciplinary action, up to and including summary dismissal.
24.5.7
Users must ensure that business critical information is not stored solely within the Email system.  The Email system
should not be used as a primary filing system and users should follow archiving guidelines to ensure system performance
remains optimal.  Network locations with specific assess rights are available for storing electronic documents that
other users may need to access.  If necessary, documents must be password protected.

**24.5.8** Any Employee who attempts to disable, defeat or circumvent any Company security facility (e.g. a firewall, anti-virus

application etc.) without authority, will be subject to disciplinary action.
24.5.9
Employees who feel that they have cause for complaint as a result of any electronic communication should raise the
matter initially with their Line Manager and/or Human Resources.  If necessary, the complaint can then be raised
through the Grievance Policy and Procedure.
