# Section 11: Data Protection at Work


## 11.1 Purpose and Scope

11.1.1  The Company recognises that Employees may come into contact with and use confidential information about people,
including suppliers, customers and Employees, as part of their contractual role.
11.1.2  The purpose of this Policy and Procedure is to ensure that in such cases, the standards of behaviour and confidentiality
established by the Company and the Regulation (EU) 2016/679 of the European Parliament and of the Council (also
known as the “GDPR”) and the UK Data Protection Act 2018 are transparently and consistently maintained.

## 11.2 Policy Principles

11.2.1  The Company fully supports and complies with the GDPR Regulation and the UK Data Protection Act 2018. The
Company Secretary is responsible for the Company’s Data Protection compliance and is registered as the Company’s
Data Protection Officer.
11.2.2  Where an Employee is in any doubt about information they are using, the Company strongly recommends Employees
seek advice from their Line Manager or the Company’s Data Protection Officer.
11.2.3   The GDPR Regulation and the UK Data Protection Act 2018 require that eight principles are adhered to in the handling
of personal data:
- Be lawfully processed;
- Be processed for limited purposes and not in any manner incompatible with those purposes;
- Be adequate, relevant and not excessive;
- Be accurate;
- Not be kept for longer than is necessary;
- Be processed in accordance with individual’s rights;
- Be secure;
- Not be transferred to countries without adequate protection.

The lawful bases for processing are set out in Article 6 of the GDPR. At least one of these must apply whenever you
process personal data:
- **a)** Consent: the individual has given clear consent for you to process their personal data for a specific purpose.
This basis will only apply to any items which do not fall within the other headings listed below.
- **b)** Contract: the processing is necessary for a contract you have with the individual, or because they have asked
you to take specific steps before entering into a contract. This basis will apply for the processing of most data
held on each employee.
- **c)** Legal obligation: the processing is necessary for you to comply with the law (not including contractual
obligations). This basis applies for any data which we are required to submit to regulatory authorities, such
as HMRC, relating to taxation and National Insurance or similar items such as processing to comply with
attachment of earnings orders.
- **d)** Vital interests: the processing is necessary to protect someone’s life.
- **e)** Public task: the processing is necessary for you to perform a task in the public interest or for your official
functions, and the task or function has a clear basis in law.
- **f)** Legitimate interests: the processing is necessary for your legitimate interests or the legitimate interests of
a third party unless there is a good reason to protect the individual’s personal data which overrides those
legitimate interests.
11.2.4  The GDPR Regulation and the UK Data Protection Act 2018 apply to all personal data, where computerised, manual or
any other format, as long as the data is in a system that allows the information to be readily accessible.
11.2.5  Where sensitive information is held, Employees will be given code words or passwords. Employees must ensure these
passwords are not mislaid or divulged to other Employees or outside agencies, as detailed in the Email and Internet
Policy and Procedure.


11.2.6  Deliberate or fraudulent access to unauthorised information, such as, but not exclusively, Employee records, will be
treated as Gross Misconduct and dealt with under the Company’s Disciplinary Procedures.

## 11.3 Records Held and Access to your Personal Data

11.3.1  The Company holds personal data on each Employee. The Company will usually hold records in accordance with the
recommendations by the Information Commissioner in its Employment Practices Data Protection Code. The general
nature of this information is as indicated below.
11.3.2  Where an Employee wishes to gain access to their Personal Data, they have the right to request copies of information.
11.3.3  The Employee must provide written details of the data they wish to have a copy of, the reasons for this access and the
intention of the Employee once the data has been provided.

**11.3.4**
The mechanism to do so is that they make a subject access request by following the link included on the company’s
website or by sending a written requests for access to Personal Data must be made to the Company Secretary.

**11.3.5**
The Company has up to thirty days to respond to any request for access to personal data.

## 11.4 General Data Protection Regulation


**11.4.1**
Privacy Notice: As an Employer the Company needs to keep and process information about Employees for normal
employment purposes.  The information that is held and processed will be used for management and administrative
purposes only.  The Company will keep and use it to enable us to run the business and manage our relationship with
the Employee effectively, lawfully and appropriately, during the recruitment process, whilst the Employee is working
for the Company, at the time when employment ends and after the Employee has left.  This includes using information
to enable the Company to comply with the employment contract, to comply with any legal requirements, pursue the
legitimate interests of the Company, and protect the Company’s legal position in the event of legal proceedings.  If
the Employee does not provide this data, the Company may be unable, in some circumstances to comply with its
obligations and will tell the Employee the implications of that decision.

**11.4.2**
As a Company, we may sometimes need to process Employee’s data to pursue our legitimate business interests, for
example, to prevent fraud, administrative purposes or reporting potential crimes.  The Company will never process the
Employee’s data where these interests are overridden by the Employee’s interests.

**11.4.3**
Much of the information that the Company holds on the Employee will have been provided by the Employee, but
some may come from other internal sources, such as the Employee’s Line manager, or in some cases, external sources,
such as employment agencies, occupational health and referees.

**11.4.4**
The sort of information that the Company holds includes the Employee’s application form and/or CV, references,
contract of employment and any amendments to this, correspondence with or about the Employee, for example
letters to the Employee about a pay rise or, at the Employee’s request, a letter to a mortgage company confirming
salary, information needed for payroll, benefits and expenses purposes, contact and emergency contact details,
records of holiday, sickness and other absence, records relating to career history, such as training records, appraisals,
other performance measures and, where appropriate, disciplinary and grievance records.

**11.4.5**
The Employee will of course, inevitably be referred to in many company documents and records that are produced by
the Employee and their colleagues in the course of carrying out their duties and the business of the Company.

**11.4.6**
Where necessary, the Company will keep information relating to the Employee’s health, which could include reasons
for absence and GP reports and notes.  This information will be issued in order to comply with the Company’s health
and safety and occupational health obligations, to consider how the Employee’s health affects their ability to do
their role and whether any adjustments to their role may be appropriate.  The Company will also need this data to
administer and manage statutory and company sick pay and other related benefits.

**11.4.7**
In addition, the Company monitors computer and telephone use, as detailed in the [IT Systems Use Policy](24-it-systems.md) and
Procedure.  The Company also keeps records of hours of work by way of a clocking on and off system, or a register.

**11.4.8**
Other than as detailed below, the Company will only disclose information about Employees to third parties if it is
legally obliged to do so, or where the Company needs to comply with its contractual duties to the Employee, for
instance, to pass on certain information to pension or health insurance schemes.

**11.4.9**
The Company may transfer information about Employees to other group companies for purposes connected with
their employment or the management of the company’s business.
11.4.10 In limited and necessary circumstances, the Employee’s information may be transferred outside of the EEA or to an
internal organization to comply with its legal or contractual requirements.  The Company has in place safeguards to
ensure the security of the Employee’s data.
11.4.11 The Employee’s data will be stored for a period of 7 years, after the cessation of employment.
11.4.12 If in the future the Company intends to process Employees personal data for a purpose other than which it was
collected, it will provide the Employee with information on that purpose and any other relevant information and
obtain explicit consent for such use.
11.4.13 Where data falls under the definition of Special Category Data under GDPR, the company will ensure that it satisfies a
Special condition, as defined in Article 9 of GDPR.
11.4.14 Where data falls under the definition of Criminal Offence Data under GDPR, the company will ensure that it has a legal
basis, as defined in Article 10 of GDPR.

## 11.5

Employee Rights

**11.5.1**
Under the General Data Protection (GDPR) the Employee has a number of rights with regard to their personal data.
The Employee has the right to request access to and rectification or erasure of personal data, the right to restrict
processing, object to processing as well as in certain circumstances the right to data portability.

**11.5.2**
If the Employee has provided consent for the processing of their data and they have the right (in certain circumstances)
to withdraw that consent at any time which will not affect the lawfulness of the processing before the Employee’s
consent was withdrawn.

**11.5.3**
The Employee has the right to lodge a complaint to the Information Commissioners’ Office, if they believe that the
Company has not complied with the requirements of the GDPR with regards to personal data.

**11.5.4**
If the Employee has any concerns as to how their data is processed they can contact our Data Protection Officer at
the following address:- The Company Secretary, Stovax Limited or Gazco Limited (as applicable), Falcon Road, Sowton
Industrial Estate, Exeter, Devon. EX2 7LF.


## 11.6

Employee Responsibilities.

**11.6.1**
Where an employee is aware that a breach of personal data, no matter how trivial may have occurred, they must
immediately send the details to the Data Protection Officer, via the email address privacy@stovax.com, with a copy to
their line manager. In exceptional circumstances, this notification can also be made in writing to the address in

11.5.4 above.
11.6.1.1 All breaches must be notified, no matter how trivial.
11.6.1.2 Failure to comply with 11.6.1 may result in disciplinary sanction, depending on the nature of the issue concerned, up
to and including dismissal, where the breach causes extensive damage to the company’s reputation and/or results in
external financial penalties.
11.6.2.  The employee must then assist the Data Protection Officer (or deputy) in establishing the extent of the breach and
rectifying it, where relevant.
