# Section 38: Shared Parental Leave


## 38.1 Purpose and Scope


**38.1.1** The purpose of this policy and procedure is to communicate the statutory requirement so that the Company’s Shared Parental Leave Policy and Procedure is transparent and consistently applied.  The Company recognises that, from time to time, the Employees may have questions or concerns relating to their shared parental rights.  It is the Company’s policy to encourage open discussion with Employees to ensure that questions and problems can be resolved as quickly as possible.

**38.1.2** Shared Parental Leave enables eligible parents to choose how to choose how to share the care of their child during the first year of birth or adoption.

## 38.2 Contractual Benefits


**38.2.1** Employees are entitled to the benefit of their normal terms and conditions of employment, save for terms relating to salary (unless their contract states otherwise), throughout their Shared Parental Leave.

## 38.3 Eligibility


**38.3.1** The right to Shared Parental Leave applies to all Employees who fulfil the relevant eligibility criteria.  The right allows the mother to choose to bring her maternity leave to an end at any point after the initial two weeks (or four weeks for factory/manual staff) compulsory maternity leave period following the birth of the child.  Parents can then choose how to split up the remaining weeks of leave between them.  Shared Parental Leave can be taken by each parent separately or at the same time.

**38.3.2** Mothers will continue to be entitled to 26 weeks Ordinary and 26 weeks additional [Maternity Leave](28-maternity-pregnancy.md) as they are now. Mothers rights during maternity will remain the same as will maternity pay entitlements.

**38.3.3** Mothers can elect to bring maternity leave to an end early and opt into a period of Shared Parental Leave and

pay instead.

**38.3.4** Ordinary [Paternity Leave](34-paternity.md) must be taken during the 8 weeks following the birth of the child (but is not compulsory) and may not be shared with the mother.

**38.3.5** Shared Parental Leave is also available to a father where the mother works but does not qualify for Statutory [Maternity Leave](28-maternity-pregnancy.md), such as where she is self-employed.

**38.3.6** In order to qualify for Statutory Shared Parental Leave the following criteria must be met:
- Shared Parental Leave will apply to parents of children due on or after 5th April 2015
- Employees must have been continuously employed for at least 26 weeks by the end of the 15th week before the expected week of childbirth
- Employees must remain continuously employed until the week before any period of Shared Parental Leave starts
- Employees must have, to expect to have, the main responsibility for the caring of the child and must be the mother/adopter and either the father of the child (in the case of birth) or the spouse, civil partner or partner of the child’s mother/adopter.
- The mother has curtailed her entitlement to [Maternity Leave](28-maternity-pregnancy.md) or has returned to work
- The Employee has provided evidence to the Company, this may include a copy of the Birth Certificate or a declaration from the parents confirming the date and place of birth if the birth certificate is not yet available, and the name and address of the other parent’s employer.

## 38.4 Amount of Shared Parental Leave Available


**38.4.1** The maximum amount of Shared Parental Leave that can be shared between the parents is 50 weeks. The leave can be taken during the 12 months following the birth of the child, but cannot begin earlier than 2 weeks (or 4 weeks for factory/manual workers) following the child’s birth.

**38.4.2** Shared Parental Leave only becomes available once the mother has given notice to end her entitlement to [Maternity Leave](28-maternity-pregnancy.md) early.



**38.4.3** The Shared Parental Leave can be taken separately, or at the same time, subject to the following requirements:-
- The minimum period of leave must be one week
- The leave must be taken in multiples of complete weeks, and
- The leave may be taken as one continuous period, or discontinuous periods.

## 38.5 Entitlement to Shared Parental Pay


**38.5.1** Up to 38 weeks of Shared Parental Pay will be available to be shared between parents.

**38.5.2** Statutory shared Parental pay is paid for the first 38 weeks at the lower of the statutory prescribed rate, or 90% of the relevant parent’s normal weekly earnings (subject to the lower earnings limit).

**38.5.3** The remaining 13 weeks of Shared Parental Leave are unpaid.

## 38.6 Discussions regarding Shared Parental Leave


**38.6.1** An Employee considering/taking Shared Parental Leave is encouraged to contact HR to arrange an informal discussion as early as possible regarding their potential entitlement, to talk about their plans and to enable the Company to support the individual.

**38.6.2** The HR Department may upon receiving a notification of entitlement to take Shared Parental Leave seek to arrange an informal discussion with the Employee to talk about their intentions and how they currently expect to use their Shared Parental Leave entitlement.

**38.6.3** Upon receiving a leave booking notice, HR will usually arrange a meeting to discuss it.  Where a notice is for a single period of continuous leave, or where the request for discontinuous leave can without further discussion be approved in the terms stated in the Employee’s notice booking leave, a meeting may not be necessary.

**38.6.4** Where a meeting is arranged, it will be planned in advance.  If the initial date is not convenient then another date can be arranged.  At this meeting the Employee may, if they wish, be accompanied by a workplace colleague.

**38.6.5** The purpose of this meeting is to discuss in detail the leave proposed and what will happen while the Employee is away from work.  Where it is a request for discontinuous leave, the discussion may focus on how the leave proposal could be agreed, whether a modified arrangement would be agreeable to the Employee and the Company, and what the outcome may be if no agreement is reached.

## 38.7 Notice to end [Maternity Leave](28-maternity-pregnancy.md) for Shared Parental Leave


**38.7.1** The mother must give the Company eight weeks’ notice to end the entitlement to [Maternity Leave](28-maternity-pregnancy.md) early. This notice can be given before or after the birth.  If notice is given after the birth, the notice is binding.  If notice is given before the birth, the mother is able to revoke the notice up to 6 weeks after the birth.

**38.7.2** If one or both parents wish to take Shared Parental Leave, they must submit a written ‘Notice of Entitlement’ to the Company at least eight weeks before the start of the first period of Shared Parental Leave.  The notice should include the following:-
- The mother and father’s names and National Insurance Numbers
- Start and end date of any period of Statutory [Maternity Leave](28-maternity-pregnancy.md) taken/or to be taken by the mother
- The amount of any Statutory Maternity Pay or Maternity Allowance received, or to be received by the mother
- The expected week of child birth and/or actual date of the birth of the child
- The total amount of Shared Parental Leave available
- Confirmation that the Mother/Father is sharing childcare responsibilities
- A non-binding indication of how much Shared Parental Leave both parties intend to take and the proposed start and end dates of such leave, and
- A declaration from each parent to confirm that:- - The mother satisfies the eligibility criteria - The father satisfies the eligibility criteria - That the information is accurate and that if either parent ceases to meet the conditions of entitlement to Shared Parental Leave that they will immediately notify the Company.



**38.7.3** The parents may vary the amount of Shared Parental Leave they intend to take by giving written notice to the Company.

## 38.8 Continuous Periods of Shared Parental Leave


**38.8.1** If an Employee requests one continuous period of Shared Parental Leave, the Employee is entitled to take that leave. The Company will not refuse this request provided the Employee meets the eligibility criteria stated above.

**38.8.2** If an Employee requests discontinuous periods of Shared Parental Leave, meaning two or more periods of leave separated by work, within two weeks of the date of the period of leave, the Company may:-
- Agree to the periods of leave requested
- Propose alternative dates or
- Refuse the leave without proposing alternative dates.

**38.8.3** If the parties cannot agree the periods of leave then the Employee can either withdraw the request or take the total amount of leave requested as one continuous period of leave.

## 38.9 Variations to a Period of Shared Parental Leave


**38.9.1** Once the parties have agreed a period of leave, if the Employee wishes to amend the period they must send the Company written notice to vary the leave.  The variation notice may:-
- Vary start and end dates
- Vary the amount of leave
- Ask for a single period of leave to become discontinuous or vice versa.

**38.9.2** The notice must be given at least 8 weeks before the date to be varied and the new date.

## 38.10 Shared Parental Leave In Touch Days (SPLIT Days)


**38.10.1** Parents will have 20 SPLIT days per parent if on Shared Parental Leave.  They are in addition to the 10 Keeping in Touch Days (KIT) days which continue to be available to women on [Maternity Leave](28-maternity-pregnancy.md).

**38.10.2** The Company may, at its option, either pay the Employee for SPLITs worked or opt to operate a ‘time in lieu’ policy for the SPLITs.  In the event of the Company opting to operate a ‘time in lieu’ policy, Employees will be able to use their accrued time in lieu once they return to work.  This may help the Employee during the first few weeks back at work and ease the transition from Shared Parental Leave to work for the parents and the baby.  If the Company elects to pay SPLIT days, the Employee will be paid for these in the month they are worked.

**38.10.3** During the remainder of an Employee’s period of Shared Parental Leave, the Company may contact the Employee about the possibility of working on SPLITs.  The Employee may also write to the Company to request to work on SPLITs, however, the Employee is under no obligation to agree to attend work and the Company is under no obligation to offer the Employee SPLITs.  It is entirely the Employee’s decision and the Employee will not be subjected to any form of detriment if they do not wish to agree.

**38.10.4** Employees will lose one weeks’ SPLP for any week in which they agree to work for the Company over and above

5 SPLITs.

**38.10.5** The Company will endeavour to keep Employees updated on any changes in structure, appointments or vacancies in their department.

**38.10.6** The Company will ensure all Employees on Shared Parental Leave are invited to official Company functions.

## 38.11 Changes in Employee’s Circumstances


**38.11.1** It is possible that a parent’s circumstances may change after periods of Shared Parental Leave have been arranged. The Company may hold the Employee to the agreed arrangements in certain circumstances so that the Company will always have 8 weeks’ notice of a change in the planned arrangements.



**38.11.2** If less than 8 weeks before either parent is due to take a period of parental leave
- The parent informs their employer that they no longer meet the conditions of entitlement, and
- It is not reasonably practicable for the Company to accommodate this change in circumstances


Then the Company may still require that the parent takes a period of leave.  The leave will be treated as Shared
Parental Leave and will start on the same date that the next period of Shared Parental Leave was due to start.  It will
either end on the same end date, or 8 weeks from the date the company is informed of the change in circumstances,
whichever is earlier.

## 38.12 Abuse of the Shared Parental Leave Entitlement


**38.12.1** In the event the Company becomes aware of the following circumstances, the Company may consider disciplinary action:
- Where an Employee has knowingly made a fraudulent claim for Shared Parental Leave;
- Where an Employee has used Shared Parental Leave for purposes other than supporting, or providing care for the child.

## 38.13 Right to Return


**38.13.1** An Employee’s rights to return to the same job vary depending on the length of leave.  Employees are entitled to return to the same job if they have taken leave of 26 weeks or less, regardless of how many periods of leave they have taken.

**38.13.2** Employees who take more than 26 weeks leave have the right to return to either the same job, or a similar job, if it is not reasonably practicable to return to the same position.
