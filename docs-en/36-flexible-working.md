# Section 36: Right to Request Flexible Working


## 36.1

This document sets out the Company policy on the right to request Flexible Working in accordance with the basic
statutory rights. If Employees have any questions about this policy, they should contact Human Resources.

## 36.2 Purpose and Scope


**36.2.1**
The Company recognises that flexible working may, in certain circumstances, benefit everyone, the Company, the
Employee and their families.

**36.2.2**
This Policy is designed to ensure all Employees are aware of the Company procedure. It aims to set out the procedures
that may be used in the event of a request for flexible working.  However, the Company will review each request for
flexible working on an individual case by case basis.
36.2.3  This Policy places a duty on both the Company and the Employee. Both parties are required to follow the procedure,
ensuring due care and contemplation is made in both the application and consideration stages.

**36.2.4**
Employees are encouraged to consider and submit a request for flexible working as early as possible, as the process
may take up to 8 weeks.
36.2.5  Where changes are agreed, they will result in a permanent amendment of Contract of Employment, unless specifically
agreed otherwise.
36.2.6  Employees will not be able to make more than one request for Flexible Working within a 6-month period. 6 months
are measured from the date of each written request for Flexible Working submission.
36.2.7  This Policy applies to all permanent Employees of the Company, irrespective of seniority or department.

## 36.3 What is the Right to Flexible Working?

36.3.1  Employees have the right to make a request for flexible working.  Flexible working could include the following but is
not limited to:
- Reduced hours, including part time working;
- Condensed working hours;
- Change in working hours’ /shift patterns;
- Job sharing;
- Term time working.

## 36.4 Who Qualifies?

36.4.1  The Employee will have the right to request flexible working if:
- The Employee is a permanent Employee and is not a contractor or temporary member of staff employed via
an agency.
- The Employee has not made any previous application for flexible working within the last 6 months based on
the same underlying facts.
36.4.2.  The Procedure – Application Stage
36.4.3  If the Employee meets the eligibility criteria, as detailed in section 36.3.1 they have the right to request

flexible working.
36.4.4  The request must be dated and made in writing.  The Employee must state the application is being made under
the Statutory Right to request Flexible Working. Appendix 36.1 provides a Framework Request Form that Employees
should use when submitting a request for Flexible Working.

**36.4.5**
The application should indicate the date the Employee wishes to commence the Flexible Working.

**36.4.6**
The application should be made well in advance as the process may take up to 8 weeks.



**36.4.7**
The Employee is responsible for producing a carefully thought out application. They should set out their desired
working pattern, explaining how they meet the conditions concerned and including arguments why the working
pattern can be implemented.
36.4.8  The onus is on the Employee to be clear and as explicit as possible. Where information is limited the Employee may
be asked to expand on any points raised.
36.4.9  Where an Employee refuses to provide the further information needed, the request will be considered withdrawn, the
Employee will not then be able to make a further application for 6 months.

## 36.5

The Meeting
36.5.1  The Company has a legal duty to consider the application and establish whether it can accommodate the request.
36.5.2  Following a written request for Flexible Working, a meeting will be arranged between the Line Manager and the
Employee. Human Resources may also be present at this meeting, depending on the detail of the request for Flexible
Working. This will be within 14 days after the date an application for Flexible Working is received, unless this cannot be
arranged for reason of absence or the absence of the Line Manager who hears such requests, in which case the time
limit will be extended by a further 14 days from the Employee’s or Line Manager’s return.

**36.5.3**
Where the request is for a change in start or finish times by no more than one hour, this can be agreed by the Line
Manager at the meeting without the need for Human Resources to be present.  However, if the request is for anything
more significant the Line Manager should contact Human Resources for guidance.   In any event, all paperwork should
be provided to Human Resources.
36.5.4  The Employee has the right to accompaniment by a fellow Employee or Trade Union Representative at the meeting.
The Employee’s chosen companion can act as a witness, address the hearing to put and sum up the Employee’s case
and respond on behalf of the Employee to any views expressed at the meeting, and confer with the Employee during
the hearing.  The companion does not have the right to answer questions on the Employee’s behalf, address the
hearing if the Employee does not wish it, or prevent the Employer from explaining their case.  It is the Employee’s
responsibility to ensure that their chosen companion is willing to act as a witness and is available to attend the
meeting, with the exception of where the Employee has been suspended, and in this case, Employees should request
their witness through HR.
36.5.5  If the Employee’s chosen companion is unavailable on the proposed date of the meeting, and this is advised to the
Company in advance in writing, the meeting may be postponed by up to five working days at the Employee’s request,
subject to the Employee proposing an alternative time and date within five working days of the original date.
36.5.6  The meeting is held to enable a facilitated discussion where both parties gain a clear understanding of each other’s
needs and discuss the proposal in more detail. The meeting may also be used to discuss alternative working
arrangements, if appropriate.

**36.5.7**
The Line Manager will discuss the potential effects on job delivery and the Employee’s salary and benefits.
36.5.8  Where an Employee has made a fraudulent application the Company may consider disciplinary action.
36.5.9  In most cases the Line Manager will make a decision following the meeting, once all the information has been
evaluated. The Line Manager must write to the Employee within 14 working days confirming the outcome of

the meeting.
36.5.10  If the Line Manager has agreed to Flexible Working, the letter should confirm the new working pattern, the agreed
start date, the impact, if any, to the pay and conditions and confirmation the arrangement will be a permanent change
to the Contract of Employment.
36.5.11  The letter must explicitly state that Employees have no automatic right to revert back to their original working
conditions.  In exceptional circumstances, the Company may agree to a trial period, or a limited period of working
flexibly, the details of which should be confirmed in writing.


36.5.12  If the Line Manager has declined the request for Flexible Working, the letter should detail the business reasons for
refusal and the Employee’s right to appeal. Reasons could include, but are not exhaustive to:
- Burden of additional costs (including recruitment, equipment);
- Detrimental effect on the ability to meet the customers’ demands;
- Inability to reorganise work amongst existing staff;
- The effect of the proposed arrangements on other staff;
- Inability to recruit additional staff;
- Detrimental impact on the quality of the business;
- Detrimental impact on the individual’s or Company performance;
- Insufficiency of work during the periods the Employee has requested to work;
- Planned structure changes.

## 36.6 Appeal

36.6.1  If the Employee feels that they have had their application for Flexible Working unjustly rejected, they may appeal
against the decision to the Company Secretary or HR Director. The Employee should give written notice of their desire
to appeal, setting out the grounds on which the appeal is based.  Notice of the Employee’s intention to appeal should
be received by the Company Secretary or HR Director within fourteen days of the date of letter of rejection.
36.6.2   An appeal should be on one of the following grounds:-
- **a)** There has been a procedural irregularity, in which case details of the alleged irregularity should be given;
- **b)** Further facts/information not available or considered at the time have emerged which require consideration.  In these
cases, full details should be given.
36.6.3   Having received the grounds of the appeal, the Company Secretary or HR Director will, where relevant, ensure that
the appeal is not as a consequence of a misunderstanding of the procedure to date.  If this appears to be the case an
initial informal discussion may be undertaken to clarify matters.  In these cases, only if this proves unsuccessful, will a
formal appeal hearing be convened.
36.6.4  The appeal will, where practicable, be heard within fourteen working days of receiving the Employee’s grounds

for appeal.
36.6.5  An Appeal Hearing will take place at which the Employee will have the opportunity to explain their grounds of appeal.
The Employee will be informed at least two full working days in advance of the date, time and location of the hearing
and must take all reasonable steps to attend.  The Appeal Hearing will be conducted by the Company Secretary, a
nominated Director or Senior Manager.  The form of the Appeal Hearing will be at the discretion of the Company
Secretary, Director or Senior Manager hearing the appeal. An appeal will usually occur on the same basis as the
original meeting; in addition, it may involve a review of all the relevant documentation including the Employee’s
grounds for appeal.
36.6.6  An Employee may be accompanied to the Appeal Hearing by a fellow Employee or Trade Union Representative of the
Company if they wish. If the Employee’s chosen companion is unavailable on the proposed date of the Appeal Hearing,
and this is advised to the Company in advance in writing, the hearing may be postponed by up to five working days at
the Employee’s request and subject to the Employee proposing an alternative time and date within five working days
of the original date.
36.6.7  Following the Appeal Hearing, the decision imposed will be reviewed. The outcome of the appeal will be relayed in
writing within fourteen working days of the Appeal Hearing being held. The appeal decision will be final and cannot
be further appealed.

## 36.7 Extension to Timescales

36.7.1  There may be occasions where the Line Manager requires additional time to consider a request for Flexible Working.
Any extension to the timescales must be confirmed in writing, including a full explanation for the reasons for

this request.
