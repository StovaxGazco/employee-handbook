# Section 13: Disciplinary


## 13.1 Purpose and Scope


**13.1.1** The Company looks for and expects a high standard of behaviour from all of its Employees. The purpose of this Policy and Procedure is to ensure that those standards of behaviour established by the Company are transparently and consistently maintained.

**13.1.2** The Company has a strong set of values, that all Employees are expected to adhere to, and these are, Total Respect, Value People, Communicate Openly, Complete Integrity, Be Accountable, Customer First, Passion in all we do, Drive Innovation, and Care for the Environment.

**13.1.2** Most behaviour issues can be dealt with quickly and informally by an individual’s Line Manager who will make the individual aware of the standards expected and any improvements required. However, if an informal approach is inappropriate, either because it has already been ineffective, or because the alleged offence is considered to be more serious, it will be necessary to follow the formal Policy and Procedures set out below.

## 13.2 Disability or Medical Issues


**13.2.1** Should the Employee highlight that there is an impairment (disability or medical) which may be identified as a contributory factor, in the Disciplinary issue, the procedure will be paused for consideration and consultation with Human Resources of the next steps.  The following actions may be taken: -
- Referral to Occupational Health
-  Discussion with the Employee what reasonable adjustments, if any could be, or have been put in place.  This can include any support or development needs identified either for the Employee, or others in relation to the impairment.  Records of Reasonable Adjustments considered and put in place should be documented and kept securely.
-  Agree meetings at an appropriate date to renew the effectiveness of any reasonable adjustments have had to mitigate the disadvantage caused by the impairment.

## 13.3 Policy Principles


**13.3.1** The Company’s aim is to encourage improvement in individual conduct.  Phrases such as ‘Disciplinary Procedure’ and ‘Disciplinary Hearing’ are used in this Policy.

**13.3.2** Formal disciplinary action will usually be taken only once the matter has been fully investigated and an Investigation Report completed. In the case of gross misconduct, this may still result in immediate action being taken, including without limitation investigation, suspension or further action.

**13.3.3** At any disciplinary hearing the Employee will have the opportunity to hear the case against them, state their case and may be accompanied by a fellow Employee.

**13.3.4** If a disciplinary sanction other than termination of the Employee’s employment is imposed, Employees will be informed of what improvements are required to either correct the current disciplinary issue or prevent a future recurrence due to their behaviour, the timescale for the improvements to be made and the consequences if the improvements are not met or sustained.

**13.3.5** The Employee has the right to appeal against dismissal or other disciplinary sanction in the manner provided for below (see paragraph 13.10).

**13.3.6** This procedure may be implemented at any level if, in the Company’s reasonable opinion, the Employee’s conduct warrants it.  The level of disciplinary sanction applied from the implementation of this procedure will depend entirely on the seriousness of the Employee’s alleged conduct.

**13.3.7** Employees will not usually be dismissed for a first misconduct offence, other than in cases of gross misconduct or other aggravating circumstances.

**13.3.8** All cases of disciplinary action under this procedure will be recorded and placed in the Employee’s Personnel File and Human Resource Database. The Company will not refer to records any longer than allowed in accordance with the Data Protection Act 1998 (as amended).


## 13.4 Investigation


**13.4.1** No disciplinary action will be taken before a full and fair investigation into the matter in question has been undertaken by the Company. An appropriate Manager will promptly and thoroughly investigate any matter that is reasonably suspected, or believed, to contravene any of the Company’s policies or rules, or indicate a failure to attain that the Company’s required performance levels or which may otherwise be reasonably considered a disciplinary matter.

**13.4.2** The Employee (and any relevant witnesses) will be interviewed, and written notes of any interview will be kept. Where an investigatory meeting is held solely to establish the facts of the case, it will be made clear to the Employees involved that the investigatory meeting is not a disciplinary meeting. The Employee is not entitled to be accompanied at any investigatory meeting.  Having established the facts, the Manager investigating will decide whether to close the matter, deal with it informally or arrange for it to be handled formally under the Disciplinary Procedure.

## 13.5 Suspension


**13.5.1** At any stage in the disciplinary process, the Employee may be suspended from work on full pay whilst further investigations take place.  Suspension is a neutral act and is not considered to be a disciplinary sanction.  The decision to suspend the Employee will be notified to the Employee by the Manager who conducts the investigation, or in cases of alleged gross misconduct, by a member of Senior Management who is on site at that time.

**13.5.2** The suspension will last for no more than five working days initially, but may be renewed or extended at the discretion of the Company. Suspension will be confirmed in writing within three full working days. During the period of suspension, Employees will not be entitled to access any of the Company’s premises or records (whether written or computerised) except at the prior request, or with the prior consent of the Company.  Access will be subject to such conditions as the Company may, at its discretion, impose.

## 13.6 Disciplinary Hearing


**13.6.1** Before any disciplinary action is taken by the Company, a disciplinary hearing will be held with the Employee. Written notice of the date, time and location of the disciplinary hearing, together with details of the Manager or the Director (where appropriate) appointed to conduct the disciplinary hearing will be sent to the Employee at least two working days in advance of the disciplinary hearing, setting out details of Employee’s alleged conduct.

**13.6.2** The letter will include enough information for the Employee to understand both what is alleged to be wrong and the reasons why this is not considered acceptable.  Arrangements will be made for the Employee to view the investigation notes or other evidence prior to attending the Disciplinary Hearing. There may be some occasions where the Company withholds sensitive information, or omits information on witness statements to protect other Employees. Details of the right to be accompanied at the disciplinary hearing will also be included.  The Employee should notify the Manager holding the disciplinary hearing, or Human Resources at least 24 hours in advance, if they wish to exercise their right to be accompanied and by whom they will be accompanied.

**13.6.3** The Employee must take all reasonable steps to attend the Disciplinary Hearing. Persistent failure to attend a Disciplinary Hearing without good reason, may lead to the hearing being held and disciplinary action being taken in the Employee’s absence.

**13.6.4** The Employee is not permitted to carry out any covert recording of the Disciplinary Proceedings.

**13.6.5** At any Disciplinary Hearing, the Employee will receive a full explanation of the case against them, including access to any supporting evidence subject to clause 13.6.2 above.  The Employee will be given an opportunity to state their case and answer any allegations that have been made.

**13.6.6** Once the Manager is satisfied that a full and fair investigation has occurred, and the Employee has had adequate opportunity to state their case, an adjournment will usually be called. The adjournment will allow the Manager time to consider all of the evidence, including the Employee’s representations, and, if appropriate, assess what level of disciplinary sanction to impose. Where a disciplinary  sanction is believed to be appropriate, the Manager will take into account the individual Employee’s disciplinary and general record, their length of service, the explanations given by the Employee and the actions taken in any previous similar case.

**13.6.7** The outcome of the disciplinary hearing will usually be advised to the Employee at the disciplinary hearing, after the adjournment, and will be confirmed in writing to the Employee within ten full working days. The written notification


will also include the details of the Employee’s right to appeal. There may be times where a longer adjournment is
required, and in these cases, the Employee will be advised of the timescales, and will receive written notification of
the outcome.

**13.6.8** The Employee may be accompanied by another Employee of the Company or a Trade Union Representative, if they reasonably request, at any Disciplinary Hearing.  In addition to the Manager who is holding the disciplinary hearing, the Company will have a representative from the Human Resources department present who will keep a written record of the hearing.

**13.6.9** The Employee’s chosen companion can act as a witness, address the hearing to put and sum up the Employee’s case and respond on behalf of the Employee to any views expressed at the meeting, and confer with the Employee during the hearing.  The companion does not have the right to answer questions on the Employee’s behalf, address the hearing if the Employee does not wish it, or prevent the Employer from explaining their case.  It is the Employee’s responsibility to ensure that their chosen companion is willing to act as a witness and is available to attend the meeting, with the exception of where the Employee has been suspended, and in this case, Employees should request their witness through HR.

**13.6.10** If the Employee’s chosen companion is unavailable on the proposed date of the disciplinary hearing and this is notified to the Company in advance, the hearing may be postponed by up to five working days at the Employee’s request, subject to the Employee proposing an alternative time and date within five working days of the original date.

**13.6.11** Should Employees be unable to attend the disciplinary hearing, due to circumstances beyond their control and which were unforeseeable at the time the Disciplinary Hearing was arranged, the Company will seek to re-arrange a mutually acceptable date. The rescheduled disciplinary hearing may proceed and a decision may be taken in the Employee’s absence, if the Employee fails to attend the rescheduled disciplinary hearing without good reason.

**13.6.12** If the Employee is absent due to sickness prior to a hearing, they may be invited to meet with the Company’s Occupational Health provider to assess their fitness to attend a hearing, and to give advice on any special requirements or adjustments for the hearing.

## 13.7

No Action

**13.7.1** Where no disciplinary sanction is appropriate, no action will be taken against the Employee and written confirmation will be sent within ten full working days.  This confirmation will also state whether the disciplinary issue was found to be proven, but not of sufficient importance to warrant a disciplinary sanction; or whether the disciplinary issue was considered unproven.

## 13.8 Disciplinary Sanctions


**13.8.1** Stage 1 –  Level One Warning

If conduct does not meet acceptable standards, the Manager will, if appropriate, give the Employee a level one
warning that continued or repeated misconduct may result in further disciplinary action. A record of the warning will
be placed on the Employee’s Personnel File but will be disregarded for disciplinary purposes after six months, subject
to satisfactory conduct and performance.

**13.8.2** Stage 2 – Level Two Warning

If the offence is a more serious one, so that the misconduct is of a sufficiently serious nature to justify going straight
to a level two warning, or the misconduct continues or is repeated following a previous level one warning, or there is
a further offence of a different nature, following a previous level one warning, then the Manager will, if they believe it
to be warranted, give the Employee a level two warning, stating that if their conduct or performance does not improve
during the period specified in the warning, they may be subject to further disciplinary action. A copy of this warning
will be placed on the Employee’s Personnel File but will be disregarded for disciplinary purposes after twelve months’
subject to satisfactory conduct.

**13.8.3** Stage 3 – Final Level Warning

If the misconduct is sufficiently serious or following a level two warning, there is a further incident of misconduct
(whether or not of the same nature) within the period specified, then the Manager may give the Employee a final
level warning, which will give details of the complaint and will warn that dismissal will result if there is no satisfactory
improvement within the stated timescale.  The stated timescale will not normally be less than two weeks. A copy of


this warning will be placed on the Employee’s Personnel File, but will normally be disregarded for disciplinary purposes
after twelve months, subject to satisfactory conduct. However, in exceptional cases, or cases with a serious safety
implication, the Director may at their discretion extend the period up to five years.

**13.8.4** Stage 4 – Dismissal

If the Employee’s misconduct is sufficiently serious, or if the conduct is still unsatisfactory, following a final level
warning and the Employee still fails to reach the required standards, the Employee will normally be dismissed.  The
decision to dismiss will be taken by a Director, and may be with or without notice.

## 13.9 Additional or Alternative Sanctions



**13.8.1** The Company may, in its absolute discretion and depending on the individual circumstances of the case, consider imposing a further sanction in addition to the issue of a warning of whatever level (or as an alternative sanction to dismissal). This may result in a reduction in basic pay or could include one of more of the following; transfer, demotion, loss of seniority, loss of increment, benefits or privileges.  Such additional or alternative sanctions will be confirmed in writing to the Employee as soon as is reasonably practicable.

## 13.10 Appeals


**13.10.1** If the Employee feels that they have been unjustly disciplined, they may appeal against the decision to the Company Secretary or HR Director. The Employee should, where possible, give written notice of their desire to appeal, setting out the grounds on which the appeal is based. Notice of the Employee’s intention to appeal should be received by the Company Secretary or HR Director within five working days of the date of dismissal or imposition of a disciplinary sanction.

The grounds of appeal must be stated and should be encompassed by one of the following: -

-  The Employee considers the sanction applied disproportionate to the offence;
-  There has been a procedural irregularity in the disciplinary process;
-  There are new facts which have come to light which have a significant bearing on the proceedings.

Having received the grounds of the appeal, the Company Secretary or HR Director will, where relevant, ensure that
the appeal is not as a consequence of a misunderstanding of the procedure to date.  If this appears to be the case an
initial informal discussion may be undertaken to clarify matters.  In these cases, only if this proves unsuccessful will a
formal appeal hearing be convened.

**13.10.2** The appeal will, where practicable, be heard within ten working days of receiving the Employee’s ground for appeal.

**13.10.3** The appeal hearing will take place at the Company premises, and the Employee will have the opportunity to explain their grounds of appeal.  The Employee will be informed at least two full working days in advance of the date, time and location of the hearing and must take all reasonable steps to attend. The appeal hearing will usually be conducted by the Company Secretary or an appointed Senior Manager or Director, though this will depend on the seriousness of the matter under consideration. The form of the appeal hearing will be at the discretion of the Company Secretary, Director or Manager hearing the appeal and will depend on the disciplinary sanction which is the subject of the appeal. An appeal will usually involve a review of all the relevant documentation including the Employee’s grounds for appeal.

**13.10.4** The Employee may be accompanied to the appeal hearing by another Employee of the Company if they wish. If the Employee’ s chosen companion is unavailable on the proposed date of the appeal hearing, and this is notified to the Company in advance, the hearing may be postponed by up to five working days at the Employee’s request and subject to the Employee proposing an alternative time and date within five working days of the original date.

**13.10.5** Should the Employee be unable to attend the appeal hearing, due to circumstances beyond their control and unforeseeable at the time the Appeal Hearing was arranged, the Company will seek to re-arrange a mutually acceptable date. The rescheduled appeal hearing may proceed and a decision may be taken in the Employee’s absence, if the Employee fails to attend the rescheduled appeal hearing without good reason.

**13.10.6** The Employee’s chosen companion can act as a witness, take a note of the proceedings, may request an adjournment (which will then be at the Manager holding the appeal hearings absolute discretion) but may not answer questions or raise questions on the Employee’s behalf. It is the Employee’s responsibility to ensure that their chosen companion is willing to act as a witness and is available to attend the meeting.



**13.10.7** Following the appeal hearing, any disciplinary penalty imposed will be reviewed, and can either be removed, reduced, confirmed or, in very exceptional circumstances, where an obvious error has been made, increased, but, in this case, only after approval by a Director of the company.  The outcome of the appeal will be relayed in writing within ten working days of the Appeal Hearing being held. The appeal decision will be final and cannot be further appealed.

**13.10.8** Until the outcome of the appeal has been decided, any disciplinary sanction (including dismissal) will remain in force. If, following a successful appeal against dismissal, an Employee is reinstated, they will be reimbursed in respect of lost salary for the period between the dismissal and the date of reinstatement.

## 13.11 Gross Misconduct


**13.11.1** Any Employee who is accused or is suspected of gross misconduct will normally be suspended from work on full pay, while the Company investigates the alleged offence. If, following the disciplinary hearing, the Company is satisfied that gross misconduct has occurred, the result will normally be instant dismissal (without notice or any payment in lieu of notice).

**13.11.2** The following offences are examples (without limitation) of gross misconduct that may result in summary dismissal:

-  Theft from or fraud against the Company, its Employees, Customers or Suppliers;
-  A serious breach of confidence;
-  Unauthorised possession of Company property or use of Company facilities;
-  Unauthorised acceptance of gifts, other than items of a trivial nature, e.g. calendars, low value confectionary or similar;
-  Serious or deliberate misuse or damage of the Company’s property, other Employees’ property, Customer’s property or Supplier’s property;
-  Deliberate falsification of reports, accounts, expense claims, time sheets or self-certification form;
-  Serious insubordination including rudeness to a manager or refusal to follow reasonable

management instructions;
-  Incapability through alcohol or being under the influence of or in possession of illegal drugs;
-  Physical violence, fighting, assault or battery on another person;
-  Harassment or bullying of another Employee, whether on sexual, racial or other grounds;
-  Abuse of computer privilege, including a serious breach of the Company’s E-mail and internet

Policy and Procedure;
-  Indecent or immoral behaviour, while in the course of their employment with the Company, including accessing and / or distributing offensive or pornographic material, whether obtained from the

Internet or otherwise;
-  Misuse of the Company’s name, or bringing into disrepute;
-  A serious breach of Health and Safety rules or procedures;
-  Serious negligence which causes unacceptable loss, damage or injury to the Company or to any individual;
-  Being convicted of a criminal offence, which in the reasonable opinion of the Company demonstrates unsuitability for further employment with the Company;
-  Unauthorised disclosure of proprietary information relating to the business and/or technology;
-  Intentional or reckless failure to follow health and safety procedures, where such failure could have

serious consequences.


These examples are not exhaustive or exclusive and offences of a similar nature will be dealt with under this disciplinary
procedure.
