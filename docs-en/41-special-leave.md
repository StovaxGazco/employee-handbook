# Section 41: Special Leave


## 41.1 Purpose and Scope


**41.1.1** The Company understands that Employees may have reason to request leave other than that which is covered by any other Company Policy & Procedure.

**41.1.2** This Policy outlines the various types of Special Leave which may be granted.  It also details how requests should be made and in what circumstances the request may be agreed.

## 41.2 Requesting Special Leave


**41.2.1** Requests for Special Leave must be made to an Employee’s Line Manager by completing the Special Leave Form (Appendix 41A)

**41.2.2** As much notice as possible should be given when requesting Special Leave.

**41.2.3** When considering whether a request is agreed the Line Manager will consider the needs of the business to ensure there will be no adverse impact on the business.

**41.2.4** To support the entitlements outlined below, Employees may be required to supplement Special Leave with

Annual Leave.

**41.2.5** All reasonable requests for Special Leave will be given due consideration. However, if it is not possible to grant requests due to business requirements, and an Employee subsequently  does not attend work, this may be classed as ‘unauthorised absence’ and could lead to disciplinary action, depending on the precise circumstances.

## 41.3 Types of Special Leave


**41.3.1** The following are examples of Special Leave requests.  This list is not exhaustive: -
- Time off for Medical Appointments e.g. Hospital, Doctor, Dentist, Optician
- Urgent domestic or family issues
- Religious or Cultural observance
- Court Appearances (excluding Jury Service - For further information on Jury Service please refer to [Section 26](26-public-civic-duties.md) “Leave for Public and Civic Duties Policy & Procedure”.

## 41.4 Time off for Medical/Doctor/Dentist/Optician


**41.4.1** Hospital, Doctor, Dentist or Optician appointments should be arranged out of working hours if at all possible, or so as to give minimal disruption to the working day.  Time taken during working hours for the above appointments will be unpaid.   Employees are permitted to take time as ‘Unpaid Special Leave’, time deducted from any Flexi-Time accrued (in accordance with [Section 19](19-flexi-time.md) [Flexi Time Policy](19-flexi-time.md) & Procedure) or to make the time to be made up after the appointment, which for simplicity should also be recorded on the Flexi-Time monitor.

**41.4.2** When requesting Special Leave, the Employee should provide their Line Manager with appropriate documentation, confirming the appointment where appropriate.

**41.4.3** When the appointment requires a whole day’s absence, this can be taken as unpaid, as annual leave, or under the terms of the Flexi-Time Policy & Procedure.

## 41.5 Urgent Family or Domestic issues


**41.5.1** Employees can request Special Leave in circumstances of urgent family or domestic issues which are not covered by any of the other company policies.  Some examples would be: -
- Vehicle theft
- Vehicle accident
- Burglary
- Emergency repairs to home or services arising from, or to avoid flooding or fire.



**41.5.2** In such cases Employees will normally be granted one days unpaid Special Leave.  Employees should contact their Line Manager, when the incident occurs, which may be taken as be taken as unpaid, as annual leave, or under the terms of the Flexi-Time Policy & Procedure.


**41.5.3** At times Employees may experience personal difficulties which can be extremely stressful, e.g. relationship break-up, repossession of a home, bankruptcy, redundancy of a partner etc.  Line Managers should deal with such requests for Special Leave with discretion as each case will need to be considered on the individual circumstances.

**41.5.4** In cases where significant absence is likely, exceeding a week, then this should be referred to the appropriate director for a decision.

## 41.6 Religious and Cultural Observance


**41.6.1** There are some Employees who have particular needs for time off in relation to religious or cultural observance.  This may be on a daily or weekly basis for a small number of staff, but more often, will involve requests for a day off for a particular religious or cultural occasions, particularly if this occurs on a date which is not observed as a UK

bank holiday.

**41.6.2** All Employees who have needs for time off for religious or cultural observance will be entitled to request

the following: -
- Flexibility in the arrangements of shifts, rotas and working hours generally
- Annual Leave
- [Flexi Time](19-flexi-time.md) (in accordance with the [Flexi Time Policy](19-flexi-time.md) & Procedure)
- Unpaid Special Leave.

**41.6.3** The Line Manager will be sympathetic to requests on these grounds and will try to accommodate them, wherever it is reasonably practicable to do so, subject to the needs of the business.

## 41.7 Court Appearances


**41.7.1** Employees may be required to attend court for reasons unrelated to company business.  The Employee should utilise annual leave or time off under the terms of the Flexi-Time Policy & Procedure for this occasions.  Otherwise the Employee should apply for unpaid Special Leave.

## 41.8

All other Leave Requests

**41.8.1** Employees may request Special Leave for other reasons.  All requests should be given equal and consistent consideration on an individual basis.

**41.8.2** All Employees who require time off will be entitled to request Special Leave, subject to the needs of the business.

**41.8.3** In authorising Special Leave, Line Managers should consider:
- The needs of the business
- The notice given for the request for Special Leave.

**41.8.4** When requesting Special Leave Emplwoyees should give as much notice as possible. Line Managers should be sympathetic to requests and should accommodate them wherever it is reasonably practicable to do so.  However, if the needs of the business require, or reasonable notice is not given, then the Line Manager may have no choice other than to refuse the request for Special Leave.
