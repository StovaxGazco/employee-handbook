# Section 14: Drugs and Alcohol Abuse

14.

### Drugs And Alcohol (Substance Use) Policy And Procedure

14.1.
Purpose and Scope

**14.1.1** This Policy and Procedure is designed to ensure all Employees are aware of the standards the Company expects.

It aims to set out the standards and procedures that may be used, where alcohol or drug/substance “substance”
problems are brought to the Company’s attention. The Policy also lays out the framework of support the Company will
provide where an Employee notifies the Company prior to a ‘cause for concern’ screening.

**14.1.2** This Policy applies to all Employees of the Company, irrespective of seniority or contract type and does not discriminate

at any level.
14.1.3
Employees are reminded of the benefit of the Bupa Employee Assistance Programme benefit, and are encouraged
to seek help via this service should they be facing issues with drugs or alcohol.  More details of this can be found in
Section 29 of the Employee Handbook.
14.2.
Policy Principles

**14.2.1** The Company is under a duty to provide a safe and healthy environment for its Employees.  In this respect, the

Company is committed to promoting the general wellbeing of all of its Employees. Dependence, (whether physical,
psychological or social) alcohol, or the wrongful use of “substances” by any of its Employees not only affects their
own health, but may also affect their attendance, work performance and judgement. It may also impact adversely
relationships at work and the treatment and safety of our customers, suppliers and other employees.

**14.2.2** Under Health and Safety legislation, all Employers have a general duty to ensure the health, safety and welfare of their

Employees.

**14.2.3** Under section 7 of the Health and Safety at Work Act 1974, Employees are also required to take reasonable care

of themselves and others who could be affected by what they do.  This includes a responsibility to safeguard other
people’s health and safety against adverse impacts that could be caused by any act (or failure to act) of the Employee.
Given this responsibility, and the fact that ”substances” can create safety issues, Employees must notify an appropriate
Manager if they believe a colleague is under the influence of any ”substances”, which they reasonably believe to be a
risk to safety. Employees themselves could be liable to prosecution if their “substance” taking puts safety at risk.

**14.2.4** The Company recognises that some “substance” problems may be symptomatic of an illness, which needs to be

treated in line with other health problems. These cases would be reviewed on an individual basis.

**14.2.5** To ensure fairness and consistency for all Employees, the basic premise is that no Employee will be permitted to

consume ”substances” (other than for certified medical reasons) at work, during working hours, or be under the
influence of ”substances”.  Such an offence constitutes gross misconduct and the Employee will be liable to summary
dismissal, as detailed in the Disciplinary section of the Handbook.  Similarly, the supply of “substances” to other
third parties, whether employees, customers of supplier, on Company premises will also be considered as Gross
Misconduct.

**14.2.6** The Company has the right to search any Employee or Employee’s property should there be a reasonable suspicion

that ”substances” are being used, supplied, or are on the Company premises.  This right extends to Employee’s private
vehicles, if they are parked on Company property.

**14.2.7** On certain occasions, a Director may authorise the consumption of a limited amount of alcohol during the working

day, if appropriate for sales, marketing or entertainment reasons.  In these circumstances the Employee must remain
under the legal limit for driving as a maximum level of consumption.  Any Employee, who has had anything to drink in
these circumstances must NOT operate any Company plant or machinery until the following day.

**14.2.8** All Employees should ensure their blood alcohol level is no higher than half the limit specified by the UK’s driving

regulations (as at July 2014, this is 80 milligrams of alcohol in 100 millilitres of blood, 35 milligrams of alcohol in 100
millilitres of breath or 107 milligrams of alcohol in 100 millilitres of urine), prior to the start of their shift or the relevant
limit for the territory in which they are working. Note that the limit in Scotland and most other parts of Europe are
significantly lower.

**14.2.9** The Company actively discourages the drinking of alcohol off site during breaks within the working day, e.g. lunchtime.

Any Employee operating machinery, or driving Company vehicles is not permitted to drink any alcohol during the
working day, save as covered by Section 14.2.7 above.


**14.2.10** It is the responsibility of all Employees to be aware of the contents of the Substance (Including Drugs and Alcohol)

Policy and Procedure.

**14.2.11** This Policy will apply also to all external contractors, they will not be subject to any testing, however, if they appear to

be under the influence of “substances” they will be required to leave the premises immediately and not allowed to
work for the Stovax Group thereafter.

**14.2.12** The Company reserves the right to ask any of its Employees to undertake a random drugs or alcohol breath test at any

time upon reasonable suspicion of illegal substance use or of intoxication above the limits specified in 14.2.8 above.
Failure to agree to undertake the test, without reasonable excuse, will be considered gross misconduct and will result
in immediate dismissal.

**14.2.13** The Company considers all Employees to be potentially at increased risk if they are using certain medicines, (over the

counter, or prescribed medicines) legal drugs or illegal drugs.

**14.2.14** The Company has a zero tolerance for confirmed laboratory positive results for substances, for which no alternative

medical explanation can be found.

**14.2.15** The drugs tested for, under the standard testing programme, include, but are not limited to’ Amphetamines,

Ketamine, Morphine, Benzodiazepines, MDMA (Ecstasy), Tramadol, Cannabis (THC), Methadone, Cocaine and
Methamphetamine.

**14.2.16** It should be recognised that prescribed and over the counter medicines may also cause impairment at work.  It

is therefore the Employee’s responsibility to seek advice from their GP on any medicines they are taking prior to
continuing their work activities.

**14.2.17** Employees taking medication should inform their Line Manager and HR of any possible side effects of their medication

and assess its impact on their work.  Any medical information will be treated in the strictest confidence and will be
subject to the provisions of the GDPR Regulations as “special category” data.

14.3.     	 Procedure

**14.3.1** Problems relating to ”substance” abuse may come to light in three main ways:

- The individual may choose to seek help on a completely voluntary basis, namely the ‘voluntary referral
process’;
- The individual’s colleagues may identify signs of “substance” misuse or deteriorating performance and/or
behaviour, namely the ‘cause for concern’ process’
- Random Alcohol and other ”substance” testing.

**14.3.2** Voluntary Referral Programme: The primary objective of a ”substance” abuse referral programme is to provide effective

help and intervention, thus overcoming the problems, which combine together to encourage people to conceal or
deny the existence of the problem.

**14.3.3** The programme is preventative in nature, it is intended to remedy work performance difficulties and eliminate health

and safety risks at the earliest possible opportunity. It is in no way intended to interfere with the private lives of
Employees or their social activities.

**14.3.4** Once the Employee has advised their Line Manager or Human Resources (other than following a positive test, or

immediately prior to the undertaking of a random test), then the Company will be confidential and sympathetic in its
response.

**14.3.5** The Line Manager and Human Resources will discuss the Employee’s circumstances, aspects of work performance

and/or behaviour that may have been, or could be affected and the safety risks relating to the Employee’s role.
Following this conversation, a decision will be made about the short term suitability of the Employee continuing in
their contractual role.

**14.3.6** The interview should be confined to aspects of work performance and/or behaviour, unless there is clear evidence of

”substance” misuse or the Employee raises the matter themselves.

**14.3.7** In the event it is deemed inappropriate, or unsafe for the Employee to continue in their contractual role, the Line

Manager/ Human Resources will consider alternative roles. Where no suitable, safe, alternative role can be found, the
Employee will be placed on sick pay, until the Company considers it appropriate to return to the work place. Normal
entitlement to Company sick pay will apply.

**14.3.8** The Company may seek medical advice, from an appropriate Occupational Health Physician.  In most cases the

Employee will be required to provide written consent, allowing the Physician access to their medical files.

**14.3.9** The Occupational Health Physician will provide a report for the Company, which will include a summary of the

Employee’s condition, the potential impact on work performance, attendance and colleagues’ safety and welfare, a
suggested network of support for the Employee, a rehabilitation programme and the monitoring programme.

**14.3.10** The Company makes no commitment to fund any form of treatment or counselling.  Assessments for funding will be

made on a case by case basis.

**14.3.11** The Employee will be granted the necessary leave to undergo any help or intervention that is required and such leave

will be allowed but will be unpaid.

**14.3.12** Every effort will be made to ensure that the Employee is able to return to the same job, unless resumption of the

same job would risk undermining a return to a satisfactory level of job performance, or the safety and welfare of other
Employees.

**14.3.13** Employees who, having approached the Company to seek help because they are possibly experiencing ”substance”

abuse problems, decline to accept a referral for assistance or discontinue professional assistance, and who continue
to have an unsatisfactory level of work performance or attendance, will be subject to the Company’s Absence
Management (Sickness) or Disciplinary Procedure,  or Capability & Performance Policy & Procedure, as appropriate,
and this may lead to the Employee’s dismissal.

**14.3.14** All Employees may be subject to random screening (which will be selected without any bias or discrimination), in

addition to periodic screening, which will form part of the rehabilitation programme. Should any screening result be
positive the Company reserves the right to invoke the disciplinary procedure, which could result in disciplinary action
being taken, including the option of summary dismissal.


**14.3.15** Cause for Concern Procedure:  Work Colleagues who suspect an Employee of having a ”substance” problem should

immediately discuss the situation with the Line Manager, senior person on site or Human Resources.

**14.3.16** Reasons for raising concerns about Employees could include a combination of smelling alcohol, mood changes,

irritability, lethargy, increasing general unreliability and unpredictability, impaired concentration and memory, lack of
judgement or safety concerns.  This list is not exhaustive.

**14.3.17** When an Employee is visibly under the influence of ”substances” at work, the Manager concerned should immediately

relieve the Employee of their duties and take them to a private area on site.  The Employee may be subjected to an
alcohol or drugs test, administered by a trained member of the management.

**14.3.18** As part of an incident or accident investigation, testing may be carried out on those individuals involved, where

possible, to determine whether or not “substances” may have been a contributory factor.  These individuals must be
held separate to other witnesses and under the supervision of the responsible person (usually the Line Manager or
other nominated senior person) at all times until tested.

**14.3.19** Where the Employee’s results are positive, the Manager is responsible for ensuring the Employee is escorted home

safely, and that arrangements are made for them to be interviewed as soon as reasonably practicable thereafter.  It
is the Manager’s responsibility to ensure the Employee is not allowed to drive and they must attempt to remove the
Employee’s car keys. If the Employee does drive off the Police should be notified immediately.

**14.3.19** During the period between test result and investigation meeting, the Employee will remain suspended on full pay. The

Company will invoke the disciplinary procedure, which could result in disciplinary action being taken, including the
option of summary dismissal.

**14.3.20** Where the option of summary dismissal is not taken, the Company will follow the process as detailed at 14.3.7 to

14.3.14.

**14.3.21** Any non-negative drug test result obtained by the Company’s test procedures will be confirmed by  laboratory


analysis strictly following Chain of Custody Arrangements and documentation.

**14.3.22** Where the Employee’s results are negative the Manager is responsible for authorising the Employee’s return to work.
