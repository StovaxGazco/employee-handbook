# Section 5: Bullying and Harassment


## 5.1 Purpose and Scope


**5.1.1** It is the policy of the Stovax Group to make every effort to provide a working environment that is free from bullying, harassment and intimidation. The Company has zero tolerance of bullying, harassment and intimidation.

**5.1.2** The purpose of this Policy is to ensure that those standards of behaviour established by the Company are transparently and consistently maintained.

**5.1.3** The Policy provides a structure and forum for Employees to discuss their concerns and issues, to ensure that should any incident of bullying, harassment and intimidation be raised, it is dealt with fairly, with a focus on resolution and improving the working relationships or environment as appropriate.

## 5.2 Policy Principles


**5.2.1** The Company promotes an open and non-threatening environment. It believes everyone should be treated with dignity and respect at work; therefore, bullying, harassment and intimidation will not be tolerated in the workplace.

**5.2.2** The Company has a strong set of values, that all Employees are expected to adhere to, and these include, Total Respect, Value People, Communicate Openly, Complete Integrity, and Be Accountable.

**5.2.3** The Company recognises the potential effect on the health, confidence, morale and performance of those affected by bullying, harassment and intimidation. It may also have a damaging effect on other Employees who witness or who have knowledge of events or incidents.  Effects on the Company can include loss of morale, poor work performance, increased turnover of staff, legal claims and damage to the Company’s reputation.

**5.2.4** Different people find different things acceptable. Everyone has the right to decide what behaviour is acceptable to them and to have their feelings respected by others.

**5.2.5** These responsibilities extend beyond the workplace to any environment where work-related activities take place

(e.g. social gatherings organised by the Company).

**5.2.6** If the complainant or alleged harasser is not employed by the Company, e.g. if the worker’s contract is with an agency, this Policy will apply with any necessary modifications. For example, if the alleged harasser is not an Employee, the Company could not dismiss the worker but would instead require the agency to remove the worker, if appropriate, after investigation and disciplinary proceedings.

**5.2.7** The Policy does not cover bullying, harassment or intimidation by customers, suppliers, or visitors and, in these cases, Employees should report any such behaviour to their Line Manager, who will take appropriate action. Bullying, harassment or intimidation of customers, suppliers, visitors or others by Employees will be dealt with through the [Disciplinary Policy and Procedure](13-disciplinary.md).

**5.2.8** Appropriate disciplinary action, up to and including dismissal, will be considered with respect to Employees who contravene this policy, however, no formal disciplinary action will be taken until the matter has been fully investigated.

**5.2.9** Disciplinary action may also be taken against someone who makes an unfounded allegation of Bullying or Harassment or Intimidation, without reasonable grounds.

## 5.3 Definition of Bullying, Harassment & Intimidation


**5.3.1** Harassment, in general terms, is unwanted conduct affecting the dignity of men and women in the workplace.

It may be related to the following:
- Age
- Sex
- Race
- Disability
- Religion
- Sexual orientation
- Nationality
- Gender reassignment
- Any other personal characteristic of the individual



**5.3.2** It may be persistent or an isolated incident. Harassment occurs if the actions or comments are viewed as demeaning and unacceptable to the recipient.

**5.3.3** Bullying may be characterised as offensive, intimidating, malicious or insulting behaviour. It may also be described as an abuse or misuse of power through means intended to undermine, humiliate, denigrate or injure the recipient.

**5.3.4** The legal definition is if the behaviour “has the purpose or effect of violating the complainant’s dignity or creating an intimidating, hostile, degrading, humiliating or offensive environment.”

**5.3.5** Bullying and harassment is not necessarily face to face. It may also occur in written communications, electronic (e) mail, or phone conversation.

**5.3.6** Whatever form it takes, bullying and harassment is unwarranted and unwelcome to the individual. It can range from extremes such as physical violence to less obvious forms like ignoring someone.

**5.3.7** Examples of bullying and harassment include, but are not exhaustive to the following:
- Spreading malicious rumours, or insulting someone by word or behaviour (particularly on the grounds of age, race, sex, disability, sexual orientation and religion or belief);
- Unwelcome remarks about a person’s age, dress, appearance, race or marital status;
- Unwelcome posters, graffiti or obscene gestures;
- Pressure to participate in political/religious groups;
- Intrusion by pestering, spying, stalking;
- Ridiculing or demeaning someone – picking on them, or setting them to fail;
- Deliberate and persistent exclusion or victimisation;
- Unwelcome sexual advances which may include touching, lewd or suggestive comments or e-mails, or the display of offensive and sexually suggestive materials;
- ‘Cyber Bullying’ – the sending or posting of harmful or cruel text or images using the internet or other digital communication devices;
- Making continual threats or comments about job security without foundation.

## 5.4

The Employee’s Responsibilities

**5.4.1** All Employees have a responsibility to behave in ways which avoid the creation of a hostile working environment for themselves and their colleagues. They should play their part in making the Company’s policy a reality and be prepared to take action if they observe or have evidence that someone else is being harassed.


**5.4.2** The Employee’s responsibilities include the following:
- Being aware of how their own behaviour may affect others and changing it, if necessary – you can still cause offence even if you are ‘only joking’;
- Treating their colleagues with dignity and respect;
- Taking a stand if they think inappropriate jokes or comments are being made;
- Making it clear to others when they find their behaviour unacceptable, unless it should be obvious in advance that this would be the case;
- Intervening, if possible, to stop harassment or bullying and giving support to recipients;
- Making it clear that they find harassment and bullying unacceptable;
- Reporting harassment or bullying to their Line Manager or Human Resources and supporting the Company in the investigation of complaints;
- If a complaint of harassment and bullying is made, not prejudging or victimising the complainant or alleged harasser.

## 5.5 Resolving Incidents of Bullying, Harassment and Intimidation


**5.5.1** The Company encourages Employees not to ignore behaviour that makes them feel uncomfortable.

**5.5.2** In 2002, as part of the Employment Act, the Government introduced standard internal procedures for grievances, which included the handling of bullying and harassment.


- Informal Resolution – telling the bully or harasser their behaviour is not acceptable;
- Formal Resolution – reporting the allegations of bullying or harassment to your Line Manager and / or Human Resources;
- Investigation by Line Manager and / or Human Resources;
- Resolution;
- Appeal Process.


**5.5.3** If you are sure you are being bullied or harassed, then there are a number of options that will help the

resolution process.

**5.5.4** Keep a diary of all incidents, records of times, dates, any witnesses, your feelings etc. Keep copies of anything that you think is relevant, for instance memos, emails. Bullying and harassment is often revealed through patterns of behaviour and frequency of incidents.

**5.5.5** If the investigation reveals that the complaint is valid, appropriate disciplinary action will be taken against the perpetrator, as a matter of urgency.

## 5.6 How to Make a Complaint – Informally


**5.6.1** The first step in resolution is for the Employee to approach the bully, harasser or intimidator.

**5.6.2** The Employee should tell the person to stop whatever it is they are doing that is causing them distress; otherwise they may be unaware of the effect of their actions. If possible they should refer to specific dates, times and events.  This will help their explanation. The Employee should tell the person what behaviour they find offensive and unwelcome, and say that they would like it to stop immediately.

**5.6.3** The Employee may want to add that, if the behaviour continues, they intend to make a formal complaint to their Line Manager or Human Resources.

**5.6.4** The Employee should keep a note of the date and what was said and done. This will be useful evidence if the unacceptable behaviour continues and they wish to make a formal complaint.

**5.6.5** The Employee may find it useful to discuss the complaint with a colleague or have a colleague present during

the meeting.

**5.6.6** If you are in any doubt as to whether the incident constitutes harassment, then, in the first instance you should approach your Line Manager or the Human Resources on an informal basis.

**5.6.7** If circumstances are such that the Employee is not comfortable in approaching the bully, harasser or Intimidator, they may seek the assistance of Human Resources or their Line Manager.

## 5.7 How to Make a Complaint – Formally


**5.7.1** The main aim of any formal action will be to make sure that the harassment or bullying stops immediately and does not reoccur.

**5.7.2** It is only when an informal resolution cannot be found that formal proceedings should be initiated.

**5.7.3** All complaints made under this procedure will be treated seriously and with discretion.  Proceedings and records of any grievance will be kept as confidential as possible, but the Employee must appreciate that complaints cannot always be formally investigated on an entirely confidential basis.

**5.7.4** The Company will take all such complaints seriously and the Employee who makes a genuine complaint of bullying, harassment or intimidation will be protected and will not be penalised or victimised in any way. Employees who assist in the investigation process will receive the same level of protection from the Company.

**5.7.5** The Employee will be required to provide a formal statement detailing the actions and specific allegations. This statement should ideally include dates, times, witnesses and the impact on the Employee’s emotional wellbeing. In addition, the Employee must provide details of the date, time and conversation they had with the bully or harasser where they stated the alleged behaviour was unacceptable.



**5.7.6** All complaints raised through the Company Bullying, Harassment and Intimidation Policy and Procedure will be recorded and placed in the Employee’s personnel file. The Company will retain records in line with the Data Protection Act 1998 (as amended).

**5.7.7** As part of its investigations, the Company will:
- check whether any Employee(s) suspected of bullying or harassment has/have received previous warnings for similar misconduct (or other types of misconduct) and, if so, whether any earlier warnings are active;
- talk in confidence to any Employees who may have evidence relating to the Employee’s alleged behaviour;
- endeavour to persuade any Employees who may have been witness to the Employee’s alleged bullying or harassment, or who may have knowledge of it, to give a written statement to that effect;
- set up an interview with the Employee believed to have bullied or harassed a colleague; allowing them the right to be accompanied at the interview;
- allow Employees a full and fair opportunity to answer any allegations against them and/or explain their conduct;
- assess objectively whether the Employee’s conduct appears to have amounted to bullying or harassment;
- adopt an objective and balanced approach to the information gained as a result of the investigation;
- avoid allowing personal views about the Employee to influence the overall assessment of the conduct under review.


**5.7.8** The Company reserves the right to suspend or temporarily redeploy either the Employee suspected of bullying or harassment or the Employee raising a complaint of bullying or harassment during the investigations, if it is considered in the interests of the individual(s) or the Company to do so.

**5.7.9** Suspension in these circumstances does not constitute disciplinary action and will be on full pay.  Written confirmation of suspension will be sent within two working days. Suspension does not form part of any disciplinary action. During the period of suspension, Employees will not be entitled to access any of the Company’s premises, or Company records, (whether written or computerised) except at the prior request or with the prior consent of the Company.  Access will be subject to such conditions as the Company at its discretion may impose.

**5.7.10** As soon as possible following the conclusion of the investigation, the Company will inform the Employee suspected of bullying or harassment as to the outcome.

**5.7.11** The Company will decide at that point whether or not it is appropriate to instigate disciplinary action against the Employee. Any disciplinary proceedings will, where possible, be conducted by a different Manager from the person who conducted the investigation.

**5.7.12** In some cases, guidance and counselling can be offered to the Employee whose behaviour is considered unacceptable. Sometimes people are unaware of, or insensitive to, the impact of their actions, and counselling can help them accept the impact of their behaviour, change their behaviour or prevent further incidents.

**5.7.13** Employees complaining of the bullying or harassment will be kept informed of the general progress of the process of investigation and the outcome of any disciplinary proceedings. The Company will decide on a balance of probabilities, after considering all available evidence, whether harassment or bullying has occurred.  The Employee will receive written confirmation of the Company’s decision. For further details on the [Disciplinary Procedure](13-disciplinary.md) please refer to the section on Disciplinary Procedures in this Handbook.

**5.7.14** Where a complaint is upheld it may be necessary to relocate or transfer one party.

**5.7.15** If the Employee’s complaint is upheld, and the person found to have bullied or harassed them remains in the Company’s employment, every effort will be made to find a solution that will mean the complainant will be comfortable with future employment.  The Company will discuss the options with the Employee; these may include the transfer of the Harasser or the transfer of the complainant to another post if they wish.

## 5.8 Appeal


**5.8.1** If the allegation of Bullying or Harassment has not been resolved to the Employee’s satisfaction, they must inform Human Resources of their wish to appeal. The Appeal should be lodged in writing within five working days of Employees receiving notification of the original decision.



**5.8.2** Human Resources will then pass on a copy of the original written Grievance, the record of the Grievance Meeting and the decision, to the Company Secretary. Human Resources will invite the Employee to attend a further appeal meeting to discuss the incidents within ten working days of receiving notice of Employee’s wish the appeal.

**5.8.3** Written notice of the date, time and location of the appeal meeting, together with details of the person appointed to hear the appeal will be sent to the Employee at least two working days in advance of the appeal meeting.

The Company Secretary, or a Senior Manager or Director nominated by the Company Secretary, will usually review all
of the documentation including the Employees’ ground for appeal. The outcome of the appeal will be relayed in writing
within ten working days of the appeal hearing being held.  The appeal decision will be final and cannot be

further appealed.

Please note that the Bullying and Harassment Policy and Procedure set out above, does not form part of Employees’

contracts of employment and non-contractual elements may be changed by the Company in its absolute discretion

at any time.
