# Section 6: Capability & Performance


## 6.1 Purpose of the Policy


**6.1.1** The Company places great importance on maintaining levels of performance at an acceptable standard and the Capability & Performance at Work Policy and procedure provides a fair an objective process to enable managers to ensure that those standards are met in every aspect of the company’s operations.

## 6.2 Definition


**6.2.1** For the purposes of this policy, capability and performance is defined as: - Where an Employee is failing in a significant or persistent way to carry out their responsibilities or duties in a satisfactory manner, either due to lack of ability, inadequate training or lack of experience.  Such failings will be identified by the use of the following procedures and steps taken to improve performance.  Where such steps prove unsuccessful the Employee may have their employment terminated on the grounds of incapability.

## 6.3 Personal or Domestic Problems


**6.3.1** If personal or domestic problems are revealed or suspected as being a contributory factor in performance issues, consideration may be given to changing working arrangements on a temporary or permanent basis to support the individual in bringing their performance back to the required standard.  Any permanent changes to the role should be subject to the Employee’s agreement where there may be contractual implications.

## 6.4 Disability or Medical Issues


**6.4.1** Should the Employee highlight that there is an impairment (disability or medical) which may be identified as a contributory factor in the performance issue, the procedure will be paused for consideration and consultation with Human Resources of the next appropriate steps.  The following actions may be taken: -
- Referral to Occupational Health;
- Discussion with the Employee what reasonable adjustments, if any could be, or have been put in place.  This can include any support or development needs identified, either for the Employee or others in relation to the impairment.  Records of Reasonable Adjustments considered and put in place should be documented and kept securely.
- Agree meetings at an appropriate date to review the effectiveness any reasonable adjustments have had on mitigating the disadvantage caused by the impairment.

**6.4.2** If, having paused the procedure, the actions taken, including any reasonable adjustments have resolved the performance issue then action under this procedure will cease.  If the actions have not resolved the performance issue, then action under the procedure will continue.  If a separate procedure would be more appropriate, (e.g. the [Absence Management Policy and Procedure](02-absence-management.md)), then matters will be dealt with under the appropriate procedure.

## 6.5 Procedure


**6.5.1** Stage 1: Informal Procedure:

Where an Employee exhibits an inability to perform their duties satisfactorily, the Company will attempt to resolve the
matter informally, via a meeting between the Line Manager and the Employee.

**6.5.2** At this meeting the Line Manager will agree performance standards with the Employee and a time period over which improvement will be expected.  They will also agree how the Employee’s performance will be monitored.

**6.5.3** The Line Manager will then draft a letter to the Employee indicating the nature of their unsatisfactory performance, and how performance can be improved to the satisfaction of the Line Manager.  A copy of the letter will be retained on the Employee’s personnel file.

**6.5.4** If the Employee’s performance improves adequately over the time scale, then the process will terminate at this stage. If performance remains unsatisfactory, then the formal procedure will be invoked by the Line Manager as set out in Stage 2.



**6.5.4** Stage 2: Formal Procedure:

If it has not been possible to remedy the issue informally, or exceptionally where the issue is sufficiently serious to
warrant formal action without initial informal considerations, the Employee will  be invited, in writing to attend a
Capability and Performance Hearing with their Line Manager.  Human Resources will also be present at this hearing.

**6.5.5** The Employee will be provided with a minimum of 48 hours’ notice of a Capability and Performance Hearing, and the invitation letter will contain details of the performance concern, the standards that the Employee should be reaching, and the possible outcomes of the hearing.

**6.5.6** If the Employee decides to rely on any evidence, they should provide this in advance of the Capability and Performance Hearing.  This may include written documents and witness statements.

**6.5.7** The Employee is entitled to be accompanied by a work colleague or Trade Union Representative at all formal stages of the Capability and Performance Procedure, and should notify Human Resources of who will be accompanying them in advance of the Hearing.

**6.5.8** Areas where the Employee is deemed to be failing to perform satisfactorily will be discussed, along with actions taken by management to address these failings and whether these actions were adequate with clear performance standards set and monitored.

**6.5.9** Where the outcome of the hearing identifies that formal action is to be taken, formal remedies / action will be either: -
- A Level One Warning and Improvement Notice;
- A Level Two Warning and Improvement Notice;
- A Level Three Final Written Warning and Final Improvement Notice;
- Other remedy/action short of dismissal;
- Dismissal.


**6.5.10** The normal expectation would be that formal remedies/action will commence at Level One.  However, this will always be subject to the nature of the case and the seriousness of the performance issue.  Dismissal would only be expected as an outcome of a first formal hearing in exceptional circumstances.  Where formal action is taken, the Employee will be advised of the right of appeal.

## 6.6 Outcomes – Formal Remedies/Action


**6.6.1** Level One Improvement Notice and Warning for Unsatisfactory Performance

The Employee will receive written details of the nature of the performance issue, the improvement required, and the
length of assessment period and intervals for review, any intervention or support and the right and arrangements
for appeal.  This assessment period will ordinarily be for a period of between 1 and 3 months, to take account of
factors such as the timescales required to develop reasonable targets and for achieving and demonstrating sustained
improvement in performance.

**6.6.2** The Employee will be informed that a Manager may consider it appropriate to take further action during the assessment period.  For example, a Manager may consider extending the assessment period if the Employee requires a slightly longer timescale to fully demonstrate a continuous and sustained improvement in performance.  The Manager may also consider taking further action prior to the end of the assessment period, if the Employee’s performance is not likely to meet the targets/expectations during the original period.  In all cases, the Manager should discuss this with Human Resources.

**6.6.3** The Employee will also be informed that further action will be considered if performance is deemed not to have sufficiently improved.

**6.6.4** A copy of the Level One Warning, and Improvement Notice will be placed on the Employee’s Personnel File, but will be disregarded for Capability and Performance purposes after six months, subject to satisfactory performance.



**6.6.5** Level Two Improvement Notice and Warning for Unsatisfactory Performance

The Line Manager may decide to issue a Level Two Warning and Improvement Notice at Level Two with a longer
assessment period, depending upon the seriousness of the performance problem.

**6.6.6** The Employee will receive written details of the nature of the performance issue, the improvement required and the length of assessment period and intervals for review, any intervention or support and the right and arrangements for appeal.  This assessment period will ordinarily be for a period of between 1 and 3 months, to take account of factors such as timescales required to develop reasonable targets and for achieving and demonstrating sustained improvement in performance.

**6.6.7** The Employee will be informed that a Manager may considerate appropriate to take further action during the assessment period.  For example, a Manager may consider extending the assessment period if the Employee requires a slightly longer timescale to fully demonstrate a continuous and sustained improvement in performance.  The Manager may also consider taking further action prior to the end of the assessment period, if the Employee’s performance is not likely to meet the targets/expectations during the original period.  In all cases, the Manager should discuss this with  Human Resources.

**6.6.8** The Employee will also be informed that further action will be considered if performance is deemed not to have sufficiently improved.

**6.6.9** A copy of the Level Two Warning, and Improvement Notice will be placed on the Employee’s Personnel File, but will be disregarded for Capability and Performance purposes after twelve months, subject to satisfactory performance.

**6.6.10** Level Three Final Improvement Notice and Final Warning for Unsatisfactory Performance

If there is a failure to improve performance during an assessment period, or exceptionally the performance issue is
sufficiently serious, a Final Improvement Notice and Final Warning may be issued.

**6.6.11** The Employee will receive written details of the nature of the performance issue, the improvement required and the length of assessment period and intervals for review, any intervention or support and the right and arrangements for appeal.  This assessment period will ordinarily be for a period of between 1 and 3 months, to take account of factors such as timescales required to develop reasonable targets and for achieving and demonstrating sustained improvement in performance.

**6.6.12** The Employee will be informed that a Manager may considerate appropriate to take further action during the assessment period.  For example, a Manager may consider extending the assessment period if the Employee requires a slightly longer timescale to fully demonstrate a continuous and sustained improvement in performance.  The Manager may also consider taking further action prior to the end of the assessment period, if the Employee’s performance is not likely to meet the targets/expectations during the original period.  In all cases, the Manager should discuss this with  Human Resources.

**6.6.13** The Employee will be informed that failure to improve may lead to dismissal (or some other act short of dismissal) (See Section 6.6.15).

**6.6.14** A copy of the Final Improvement Notice and Final Warning will be kept on the Employee’s Personnel file, but will be disregarded for Capability and Performance Purposes after twelve months, subject to satisfactory performance.

**6.6.15** Other Remedy/Action Short of Dismissal

If there is still further failure to improve performance or exceptionally the performance is sufficiently serious to
warrant it, dismissal or some other action short of dismissal may be issued.  Action short of dismissal may include: -
- Demotion;
- Changes to terms of employment;
- No pay progression for a specified period where this could otherwise apply.

**6.6.16** If some action short of dismissal is determined, the Employee will receive written details, will be warned that dismissal could result if there is no satisfactory improvement, and will be advised of the right of appeal.

**6.6.17** In the case of action short of dismissal, a copy of the written details will be kept on the Employee’s Personnel File, but will be disregarded for Capability and Performance Purposes after twelve months, subject to satisfactory performance.



**6.6.18** Dismissal

In reaching a decision to dismiss, careful consideration of other options will be given.  If, on balance, other sanctions
are deemed inappropriate due to the seriousness of the case, and the Employee is to be dismissed, the Employee
will be provided in writing, with reasons for dismissal, the date on which employment will terminate and the right of
appeal and arrangements.

**6.6.19** Dismissal will usually be with notice, or payment in lieu of notice.

## 6.7 Improvement Notes and Reviews


**6.7.1** An improvement note will contain details of the aspects of the role requiring improvement, how these aspects of the role will be monitored, any support identified and timescales for improvement.  The note will also outline when and how often the review meetings will take place, and who else shall be involved.

## 6.8 Other Provisions


**6.8.1** During formal proceedings, including appeals, the Employee may be accompanied by another Employee of the Company if they wish.  If the Employee’s chosen companion is unavailable on the proposed date of the hearing and this is notified to the Company in advance, the hearing may be postponed by up to five working days at the Employee’s request and subject to the Employee proposing an alternative time and date within five working days of the original date.

**6.8.8** Should the Employee be unable to attend the hearing, due to circumstances beyond their control and unforeseeable at the time the hearing was arranged, the Company will seek to rearrange a mutually acceptable date.  The rescheduled hearing may proceed and a decision may be taken in the Employee’s absence, if the Employee fails to attend the rescheduled hearing without good reason.

**6.8.9** The Employee’s chosen companion can act as a witness, take a note of the proceedings, may request an adjournment, (which will then be at the Director or Manager holding the hearing’s absolute discretion), but may not answer questions or raise questions on the Employee’s behalf.  It is the Employee’s responsibility to ensure that their chosen companion is willing to act as a witness and is available to attend the hearing.

**6.8.10** If the Employee is absence due to sickness prior to a hearing, they may be invited to meet with the Company’s Occupational Health provider to assess their fitness to attend a hearing, and to give advice on any special requirements or adjustments for the hearing.

## 6.9 Appeals


**6.9.1** An Employee’s written outcome letter will include details of the arrangements to follow should they wish to appeal against a decision taken at a Capability and Performance Hearing.

**6.9.2** The appeal should be within seven calendar days of receipt of the written outcome letter.  Should the Employee require additional time to submit an appeal, they may make a request to Human Resources for a reasonable extension.

**6.9.4** The grounds of appeal must be stated in writing and should be encompassed by one of the following: -
- The Employee considers the sanction applied disproportionate to the offence;
- There has been a procedural irregularity in the disciplinary process;
- There are new facts which have come to light which have significant bearing on the proceedings.

**6.9.5** The Company will, as far as reasonably practical, seek to hear the appeal within 10 days of receipt of the submitted appeal.

**6.9.6** The Appeal Hearing will take place at the Company’s premises, and the Employee will have the opportunity to explain their grounds of appeal.  The Employee will be informed at least two full working days in advance of the date, time and location of the hearing and must take all reasonable steps to attend.  The Appeal Hearing will usually be conducted by the Company Secretary, or an appointed Director, although this will depend on the seriousness of the matter under consideration. The form of the Appeal Hearing will be at the discretion of the Company Secretary or Director hearing the appeal and will depend on the disciplinary sanction which is the subject of the appeal.  An appeal will usually involve a review of all the relevant documentation including the Employee’s grounds for appeal.



**6.9.7** The Employee may be accompanied to the Appeal Hearing by another Employee of the Company or Trade Union Representative if they wish.  If the Employee’s chosen companion is unavailable on the proposed date of the Appeal Hearing, and this is notified to the Company in advance, the hearing may be postponed by up to five working days at the Employee’s request and subject to the Employee proposing an alternative time and date within five working days of the original date.

**6.9.8** Should the Employee be unable to attend the Appeal Hearing, due to circumstances beyond their control and unforeseeable at the time the Appeal Hearing was arranged, the Company will seek to rearrange a mutually acceptable date.  The rescheduled Appeal Hearing may proceed and a decision may be taken in the Employee’s absence, if the Employee fails to attend the rescheduled Appeal Hearing without good reason.

**6.9.9** The Employee’s chosen companion can act as a witness, address the hearing to put and sum up the Employee’s case and respond on behalf of the Employee to any views expressed at the meeting, and confer with the Employee during the hearing.  The companion does not have the right to answer questions on the Employee’s behalf, address the hearing if the Employee does not wish it, or prevent the Employer from explaining their case.  It is the Employee’s responsibility to ensure that their chosen companion is willing to act as a witness and is available to attend the meeting, with the exception of where the Employee has been suspended, and in this case, Employees should request their witness through HR.

**6.8.10** Following the Appeal Hearing, any disciplinary penalty imposed will be reviewed, and can either be removed, reduced, confirmed or, in very exceptional circumstances, where an obvious error has been made, increased.  The outcome of the appeal will be relayed in writing within 10 working days of the Appeal Hearing being held.  The appeal decision will be final and cannot be further appealed.

**6.8.11** Until the outcome of the appeal has been decided, any disciplinary sanction, (including dismissal or action short of dismissal) will remain in force.  If, following a successful appeal against dismissal, an Employee is reinstated, they will be reimbursed in respect of lost salary for the period between  dismissal and the date of reinstatement.
