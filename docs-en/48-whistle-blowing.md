# Section 48: Whistle Blowing


## 48.1 Policy Statement

48.1.1  The Company encourages a free and open culture in its dealings between its Employees and all the people with
whom it engages in business and legal relations. In particular, the Company recognises that effective and honest
communication is essential, if concerns about breaches or failures are to be effectively dealt with and the Company’s
success ensured.
48.1.2  This Policy is designed to provide guidance to all those who work with, or within the Company, who may from time to
time feel that they need to raise certain issues relating to the Company with someone in confidence.
48.1.3  The detailed provisions of these disclosures have been formulated in such a way as to conform with the Public Interest
Disclosure Act 1999, and in recognition of the need to investigate allegations fully, while dealing fairly with

anyone accused.
48.1.4  The Public Interest Disclosure Act 1999 (“the Act”), gives legal protection to Employees against being dismissed or
otherwise disciplined by their Employer, as a consequence of raising, with appropriate senior personnel, serious
concerns, which they believe indicate malpractice or wrong doing within the Company.

## 48.2 Scope and Application

48.2.1  This Policy will apply in cases where Employees genuinely, and in good faith, believe that one of the following sets of
circumstances is occurring, has occurred or may occur within the Company:
- A criminal offence has been committed, is being committed, or is likely to be committed;
- A person has failed, is failing, or is likely to fail to comply with any legal obligation to which
he or she is subject;
- A miscarriage of justice has occurred, is occurring, or is likely to occur;
- The health and safety of any individual has been, is being or is likely to be endangered;
- The environment has been, is being, or is likely to be damaged;
- Any event for which the Employee reasonably believes should be disclosed in the public interest;
- Information tending to show any matter falling within anyone of the preceding paragraphs has been, is being,
or is likely to be deliberately concealed.
48.2.2  It is not necessary that Employees prove the breach or failure that they are alleging has occurred, or is likely to
occur, but may simply raise a reasonable suspicion. However, they should note that they will not be protected from
the consequences of making such a disclosure if, by doing so, they commit a criminal offence, or if they make the
disclosure motivated by a reason which is not in good faith.

**48.2.3**
Any complaints about an Employee’s Personal Terms of Employment should be raised as a Grievance under the
[Grievance Policy and Procedure](21-grievance.md) (section 19).  Such a disclosure is not   protected by the Public Interest Disclosure in
48.2.1 above.
48.2.4  If Employees wish to raise, or discuss any issues which might fall into the above category, they should contact Human
Resources or the Company Secretary, who will treat the matter in confidence.  In such cases, it is likely that further
investigation will be necessary and Employees may be required to attend an investigative hearing, as a witness or in
exceptional cases, a disciplinary hearing as a witness. Appropriate steps will be taken to ensure that their working
environment and/or working relationship(s) is/are not prejudiced by the fact of the disclosure.
48.2.5  If Employees reasonably believe that the relevant failure (i.e. one of the set of circumstances listed above under clause
48.2.1 relates wholly (or mainly) to the conduct of a person other than their Employer, or any other matter for which
a person other than the Company has legal responsibility, then they should make that disclosure to that other person.
48.2.6  While the Company hopes that such disclosures will never be necessary within its organisation, it also recognises that
it may find itself in circumstances which are new to it and each case will be treated on its own facts.
48.2.7  The Company recognises that there may be matters that cannot be dealt with internally and where external authorities
will need to be involved, and accordingly reserves the right to involve such external authorities, if appropriate.


## 48.3 Procedure

48.3.1  Concerns should initially be reported to Human Resources or the Company Secretary whose principal initial purpose
will be to establish whether or not there are prima facie grounds for the concern or complaint, and if so, the appropriate
means for dealing with it. An Employee is entitled to request another Employee be present when they make

the disclosure.
48.3.2  Human Resources or Company Secretary will, where relevant, ensure that the disclosure is not as a consequence of a
misunderstanding.  If this appears to be the case an initial informal discussion may be undertaken to clarify matters.

**48.3.3**
Human Resources or Company Secretary, will investigate the concern or allegation that has been raised and will
prepare a report within 14 days for the Managing Director, whose decision in the matter will be final.  The Company
Secretary may appoint another person to undertake the detailed investigation on their behalf, provided that the
conclusions of the person so appointed are reported to, and endorsed by, the Company Secretary before a report is
made to the Managing Director.
48.3.4  Where the matter to be raised concerns the Company Secretary, it should be raised with the Managing Director,
who will similarly investigate and report to the Board. The Board may also appoint another person to conduct the
investigation and report.
48.3.5  Where an allegation concerns the Managing Director or any person acting with their powers under delegated authority,
the Company Secretary will report to the Board.
48.3.6  If, on preliminary examination, the matter is judged to be wholly without substance or merit, the matter may be
dismissed. The complainant will be so informed and given the opportunity to refer it to the Board, who will take such
steps deemed necessary to review the case, and will communicate the outcome of the review to the complainant.
48.3.7  The person or persons who are implicated by the matter raised must be informed of the circumstances, and of the
evidence supporting any allegation made, and must be allowed to comment before any investigation is concluded.
All matters, including those dismissed after preliminary examination, and the results of their investigation, must be
reported to the Managing Director.  The outcome of all investigations into the matters raised must also be reported to
the complainant.
48.3.8  If the investigation discloses a prima facie case that the matter raised is justified, immediate action will be taken
according to established procedures (e.g. disciplinary) or by appropriate managerial intervention.
48.3.9  In the conduct of the initial investigation of any matters raised, all reasonable steps will be taken not to breach
confidentiality, or to reveal the identity of the complainant until a formal investigation is initiated.  Thereafter,
confidentiality will be maintained, in so far as it is consistent with a fair investigation, and with the right of the person
or persons being investigated to be aware of the nature of the matter raised.
48.3.10  If the matter that concerns you is serious, and you have exhausted the Company’s internal procedures, or you do
not have confidence in the Company’s actions to investigate the matter, you may contact the appropriate regulating
authority relevant to the matter in question or the Manager.
48.3.11  The Company undertakes that no Employee who makes a bona fide report under this procedure will be subjected to
any detriment as a result, in accordance with 548B of the Employment Rights Act 1996.  In the event that you believe
you are being subjected to a detriment by any person within the Company as a result of your decision to invoke this
procedure you must inform a Director immediately and appropriate action will be taken to protect you from any
reprisals.
48.3.12  All Employees must assist the Company in complying with 48.3.11 above by refraining from any adverse treatment of
a Whistleblower.
