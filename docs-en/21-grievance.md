# Section 21: Grievance

21.

### Grievance Policy And Procedure

21.1.
Purpose & Scope

**21.1.1** The Company recognises the importance of maintaining good working relationships, and it seeks to achieve a working

environment in which problems can be discussed and easily resolved at an early stage, by encouraging open and
honest communication. The Grievance Procedure does not therefore seek to replace normal day to day interaction,
discussion and feedback between the Company and its Employees.
21.1.2
The purpose of the Grievance Procedure is to ensure that any complaint made by an Employee is dealt with
professionally and consistently. The procedure provides a structure and forum for Employees to discuss their concerns
and issues which it is intended should ensure any grievance raised is dealt with fairly, with a focus on resolution and
improving the working relationships or environment, as appropriate. Every attempt will be made to resolve the matter
in an open and timely manner. All Company Employees (and in some circumstances former Employees) have the right
to seek individual redress for any grievance that relates to their employment with the Company.
21.1.3
In some circumstances, it may not be appropriate for this procedure to be used:
- If an Employee’s grievance relates to matters which are being investigated or are connected with current
disciplinary proceedings or any disciplinary sanction that has been imposed against that Employee, the
Employee should usually follow the separate disciplinary appeals procedure;
- The Company has a separate procedure for dealing with complaints of harassment or bullying, and Employees
should refer to that procedure if they have such a complaint;
- This procedure will not apply in respect of any collective disputes.  Please refer to Human Resources for
further information or guidance if you think this might be the case;
- The Company has a Public Interest Disclosure Policy for dealing with Employee’s concerns relating to
malpractice within the Company and Employees should refer to that procedure if they have such a concern;
- The events giving rise to the grievance took place over 12 months previously and there are no exceptional
circumstances justifying the delay in raising the grievance. The decision of the Company Secretary will be final
in determining whether an historic grievance will be permitted.

Please contact Human Resources if you have any queries in relation to this procedure.
21.2.
Policy Principles

**21.2.1** The aim of the procedure is to deal with any grievance at an appropriate level, within a reasonable timescale and to

the satisfaction of all those involved.

**21.2.2** All complaints made under this procedure will be treated seriously and with discretion.  Proceedings and records of

any grievance will be kept as confidential as possible, but Employees must appreciate that complaints cannot always
be formally investigated on an entirely confidential basis.

**21.2.3** All grievances raised through the Company Grievance Procedure will be recorded and placed in the Employee’s

personnel file. A copy of the Company’s relevant records will be supplied to the Employee, as part of the grievance
process. The Company will retain records in line with the Data Protection Act 2098 (as amended).

**21.2.4** Every effort will be made to hold a meeting to discuss the Employee’s grievance or appeal and to provide a decision,

within the relevant timescales provided under this procedure. However, if the matter is a complex one or if it requires
detailed investigation, or if the relevant personnel are unavailable, it may not be possible to meet those timescales.
In these circumstances, the Employee will be informed of the reason(s) for the delay, and the timescale in which a
meeting will be held or a decision can be expected.

**21.2.5** The Employee must take all reasonable steps to attend any meeting arranged to discuss their grievance.  A representative

from Human Resources will also be present and will keep a record of what is discussed and any outcomes agreed at
the meeting.

**21.2.6** An Employee may be accompanied (if he/she reasonably requests) to any meeting at which their grievance is

discussed with them, and at any subsequent appeal hearing, by another Employee of the Company or Trade Union
Representative in line with the statutory right to be accompanied.  The Company may, at its discretion, allow an
Employee to bring a companion who is not an Employee (for example, a member of the Employee’s family) where this
will help overcome a particular difficulty caused by a disability, or where the Employee has difficulty

understanding English.


**21.2.7** It is the Employee’s responsibility to ensure that their chosen companion is willing to accompany them and is available

to attend the meeting. If the Employee’s chosen companion is unavailable on the proposed date of the meeting, the
meeting may be postponed by up to five working days at the Employee’s request provided this is done in advance of
the meeting in writing.
21.2.8
If the Employee is absence due to sickness prior to a hearing, they may be invited to meet with the Company’s
Occupational Health provider to assess their fitness to attend a hearing, and to give advice on any special requirements
or adjustments for the hearing.

**21.2.9** The Company will retain the following information;

- The nature of the grievance received;
- A copy of the written grievance;
- The actions taken, including copies of minutes;
- The reasons for the action;
- Details of any appeal, if requested, including minutes and the outcome.
21.3
Disability or Medical Issues
21.3.1
Should the Employee highlight that there is an impairment (disability or medical) which may be identified as a
contributory factor, in the Disciplinary issue, the procedure will be paused for consideration and consultation with
Human Resources of the next steps.  The following actions may be taken: -
- Referral to Occupational Health
- Discussion with the Employee what reasonable adjustments, if any could be, or have been put in place.  This
can include any support or development needs identified either for the Employee, or others in relation to the
impairment.  Records of Reasonable Adjustments considered and put in place should be documented and
kept securely.
- Agree meetings at an appropriate date to renew the effectiveness of any reasonable adjustments have had to
mitigate the disadvantage caused by the impairment.
21.4.
Procedure

**21.4.1** Stage 1 - Informal


Where possible, the Employee should first raise any complaint or grievance connected with their employment by
discussing it informally with their Line Manager. As an alternative, and if the complaint relates to the Employee’s Line
Manager, the Employee can discuss the matter on an informal basis with Human Resources or a more Senior Manager.
Where the Employee’s complaint cannot be resolved informally, it should be raised using the formal procedure set out
below.

**21.4.2** Stage 2 - Formal


If an Employee’s complaint remains unresolved, or if the Employee was unable to raise the matter informally, the
Employee should set out their grievances in writing and send it to the Human Resources Director.  The Human
Resources Director will then provide a copy to the Employee’s Line Manager, who will be responsible for dealing
with the grievance. If the problem relates to the Line Manager’s conduct, the grievance will be dealt with by the next
appropriate Manager who shall be appointed by the Company.

**21.4.3** Human Resources will invite the Employee to attend a meeting with the relevant Manager to discuss the grievance

within 10 working days of receiving the Employee’s written complaint. Written notice of the date, time and location
of the grievance meeting, together with details of the person appointed to hear the grievance will be sent to the
Employee at least two working days in advance of the meeting. The Manager may carry out such investigations as he/
she considers appropriate prior to the meeting. This may involve interviewing any witnesses, if appropriate.

**21.4.4** Following the meeting, the Manager may carry out such further investigation as they consider appropriate. The

Manager will prepare a summary of the Employee’s complaint, their decision and the solution(s) proposed (if any).
The Manager may hold a meeting to give the Employee this information, but in all cases a copy of this document will
be provided to all concerned within ten working days of the meeting, and a copy will be placed on the Employee’s
personal file. The Employee shall be notified of the right to appeal against the decision, if they are not satisfied with it.


**21.4.5** Stage 3 - Appeal


If the grievance has not been resolved to the Employee’s satisfaction at Stage 2 of this procedure, the Employee must
inform Human Resources if they wish to appeal. The appeal should be lodged in writing within five working days of the
Employee receiving notification of the original decision.

**21.4.6** Human Resources will then pass on a copy of the original written grievance, the record of the grievance meeting and

the decision, to the Company Secretary or HR Director. Human Resources will invite the Employee to attend a further
appeal meeting to discuss the grievance within ten working days of receiving notice of the Employee’s wish to appeal.

**21.4.7** Written notice of the date, time and location of the appeal meeting, together with details of the person appointed to

hear the appeal will be sent to the Employee at least two working days in advance of the appeal meeting. There may
be some occasions where the Company withholds sensitive information, or omits information on witness statements,
to protect other Employees.

**21.4.8** The Company Secretary, Director or Senior Manager appointed by the Company to hear the appeal will inform the

Employee in writing of the Company’s final decision within five working days of the meeting.  The Employee will have
no further right of appeal.
21.5.
Modified Procedure

**21.5.1** An Employee may not lodge a grievance after his/her employment has ended. The Employee should contact Human

Resources in the first instance and ensure that they set out in writing the grievance and the basis of it.  The Company
will appoint an appropriate person to consider the grievance who will confirm their decision in writing to the Employee
within twenty-eight days of receiving notice of the Employee’s grievance.

**21.5.2** There is no right of appeal against the Company’s decision on a grievance brought under this procedure.


**21.5.3** Note that the grievance procedure set out above does not form part of the Employee’s contract of employment and

may be changed by the Company in its absolute discretion at any time.
21.6
Appeals
21.6.1
Employees have the right to appeal (except under the Modified Procedure - see paragraph 20.5) against the Company’s
decision in respect of their grievance, as provided for below.
21.6.2
An appeal should be on one of the following grounds: -
- **a)** There has been a procedural irregularity, in which case details of the alleged irregularity should be given;
- **b)** Further facts/information not available or considered at the time have emerged which require consideration.
In these cases, full details should be given.
21.6.3
Having received the grounds of the appeal, the Company Secretary or HR Director will, where relevant, ensure that
the appeal is not as a consequence of a misunderstanding of the procedure to date.  If this appears to be the case an
initial informal discussion may be undertaken to clarify matters.  Only if this proves unsuccessful will a formal appeal
hearing be convened.
