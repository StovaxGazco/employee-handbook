# Section 2: Absence Management (Sickness)


## 2.1 About this Policy


**2.1.1**
This Sickness Absence Policy sets out the Company’s procedures for reporting sickness absence, and for the
management of sickness absence in a fair and consistent way.

**2.1.2**
Sickness absence can vary from short intermittent periods of ill-health to a continuous period of long-term absence
and have a number of different causes (for example, injuries, recurring conditions, or a serious illness requiring
lengthy treatment).

**2.1.3**
The Company wishes to ensure that the reasons for sickness absence are understood in each case and investigated
where necessary.  In addition, where needed and reasonably practicable, measures will be taken to assist those who
have been absent by reason of sickness, to return to work, including phased hours or reduced duties.

**2.1.4**
This policy applies to Employees.  It does not apply to agency workers, consultants, or self-employed contractors.

**2.1.5**
This policy does not form part of any Employee’s contract of employment and the Company may amend it at any times.

## 2.2 Employee’s Responsibilities


**2.2.1**
The Employee has a responsibility to assist the Company in achieving its attendance targets, these include
the following: -


**2.2.2**
The Employee has a responsibility to keep their absence to a minimum.

**2.2.3**
Employees have a responsibility to maintain reasonable health standards, to support a safe working environment for
themselves and their colleagues, and to take reasonable precautions against illness and accidents.

**2.2.4**
Where possible, Employees should arrange appointments to see GPs, Dentists and Opticians outside of normal working
hours.  Where this is not possible, the Employee should agree a convenient time for the appointment with their Line
Manager.  This will usually be at the beginning or end of the day.

**2.2.5**
Where eligible, Employees may use [Flexi Time](19-flexi-time.md) to attend appointments or take the time as unpaid (this is also within
core hours for medical appointments only). Employees are also permitted to go into negative flexi-time to attend
medical appointments.

**2.2.6**
Where Employees are not  entitled to [Flexi Time](19-flexi-time.md) benefits, they should make up the time, in agreement with the Line
Manager, or take the time as unpaid.  If the time is taken as unpaid, a Special Leave Form must be completed and
passed to HR.  If time is not made up, this time will be deducted from the Employee’s salary.  If the Employee is due
overtime, the time will be deducted from the overtime owed, before the standard hours worked, otherwise it will be
depleted from the standard hours worked.

## 2.3 Disabilities


**2.3.1**
The Company is aware that sickness absence may result from a disability.  At each stage of the sickness absence
meetings procedure (set out in section 2.15 of this policy), particular consideration will be given to whether there are
reasonable adjustments that could be made to the requirements of a job or other aspects of working arrangements
that will provide support at work and/or assist a return to work.

**2.3.2**
If an Employee considers that they are affected by a disability, or any medical condition which affects their ability to
undertake their work, they should inform their Line Manager and/or Human Resources.

## 2.4 Sickness Absence Reporting Procedure


**2.4.1**
If an Employee cannot attend work because the Employee is ill or injured, the Employee should telephone their Line
Manager as early as possible, and no later than 30 minutes after the time the Employee is normally expected to start
work.  Where it is not possible for the Employee to call themselves, a family member is permitted to call on their
behalf, but only if this is not possible for the Employee to call.  The following details should be provided: -

- The nature of their illness or injury;
- The expected length of their absence from work;


- Contact details;
- Any outstanding or urgent work that requires attention.

**2.4.2**
Text messages or emails to Line Managers or colleagues are not an acceptable form of communication unless in
exceptional circumstances.  Contact should be made via the telephone.  Voice mail messages will be accepted provided
they include a number on which the Employee can be contacted.   Please be advised that failure to report absence in
the correct manner could result in disciplinary action.

**2.4.3 Managers should ensure that: -**

- Any sickness absence that is notified to them is recorded appropriately;
- Arrangements are made, where necessary to cover work and to inform colleagues and clients (while maintaining
confidentiality).

**2.4.4**
The Employee must keep in touch daily whilst they are off sick, for the first week, by telephone to update the Company
on their condition, unless they have already provided a Doctor’s note.  Thereafter a Doctor’s Note should be provided.

**2.4.5**
The Employee should expect to be contacted during their absence by their Line Manager and/or Human Resources
who will want to enquire about the Employee’s health and be advised, if possible, as to their expected return date.

It is important to keep the company advised of any return to work date.

## 2.5 Evidence of Incapacity


**2.5.1**
The Employee is able to self-certify their absence for up to 7 days (5 working days).

**2.5.2**
For absence of more than a 7 days (5 working days), the Employee must obtain a certificate from their Doctor (a
“Statement of Fitness for Work”) stating that they are not fit for work and the reason(s) why.  This should be forwarded
to Human Resources as soon as possible.  If their absence continues, further medical certificates must be provided to
cover the whole period of absence.  Fit Notes can be provided via email.

**2.5.3**
If the Employee’s Doctor provides a certificate stating that the Employee “may be fit for work” the Employee should
inform their Line Manager and/or Human Resources immediately.  The Company will discuss with the Employee any
additional measures that may be needed to facilitate the Employee’s return to work, taking account of the Doctor’s
advice.  This may take place at a ‘Return to Work Interview’ (See Section 2.13).  If appropriate measures cannot be
taken, the Employee will remain on sick leave and the Company will set a date to review the situation.

**2.5.4**
Where the Company are concerned about the reason for absence, or frequent short-term absence, the Company may
require a medical certificate for each absence regardless of duration.  In such circumstances the Company will cover
any costs incurred in obtaining medical certificates for absences of a week or less, on production of a Doctor’s invoice.

**2.5.5**
At any point during an absence, the Company may also request that the Employee meets with the Company’s
Occupational Health Provider. Notice of any appointment will be provided. (See Section 2.11).

## 2.6 Unauthorised Absence – Absence without Approval (AWOA)


**2.6.1**
Cases of unauthorised absence will be dealt with under the Company’s [Disciplinary Procedure](13-disciplinary.md).

**2.6.2**
Absence that has not been notified according to the sickness absence reporting procedure will be treated as
unauthorised absence and will normally be unpaid, except in exceptional circumstances which must be approved by

a Director.

**2.6.3**
If the Employee does not report for work and has not telephoned their Line Manager to explain the reason for their
absence, their Line Manager and/or Human Resources will try to contact the Employee, by telephone, and in writing if
necessary.  This should not be treated as a substitute for reporting sickness absence.

**2.6.4**
If the Employee does not report for work and has not telephoned their Line Manager to explain the reason for their
absence, and their Line Manager or HR have not been able to contact the Employee, HR will try to make contact with
the Employee’s emergency contact to ensure safe guarding of the Employee.

**2.6.4**
If the Company is unable to contact the Employee, and the Employee does not make contact with the Company within
48 hours.  The Company has the right to assume that the Employee has resigned from their role with immediate effect
from their last day of work, and will process the Employee as a Leaver accordingly. The Employee will receive a letter
advising them of this.


## 2.7 Sick Pay


**2.7.1**
The Employee may be entitled to Statutory Sick Pay (SSP) if they satisfy the relevant statutory requirements.  Qualifying
days for SSP are Monday to Friday, or as set out in the Employee’s Employment Contract.  The rate of SSP is set by the
Government in April each year.  No SSP is payable for the first three consecutive days of absence. It starts on the fourth
day of absence and may be payable up to 28 weeks.

**2.7.2**
There is no entitlement to paid sickness over and above SSP. Company Sick Pay is granted entirely at the discretion of
the Company.  However, where Employees have complied with the reporting procedure, it is the Company’s normal
practice to pay Employee’s during sickness absence in accordance with the following limits: -

**2.7.3 Sick pay entitlements will be paid pro-rata in line with current contracted hours**


**2.7.4**
If the Employee has been on long term sick leave continuously for more than a year, the Employee will not qualify for
Company sick pay again until the Employee has returned to work for a total of four weeks.

**2.7.5**
The Employee will retain the use of any benefits in kind, such as Company Car and Mobile Phone for the first 13 weeks,
after which they shall be at the Company’s discretion.

**2.7.6**
Any Employer and Employee pension contributions will continue, subject to the relevant scheme rules during any
period of Company sick pay or SSP.


## 2.8 Sick Leave and Holidays


**2.8.1**
If the Employee becomes sick or injured while on annual leave, such that the Employee would be unfit for work, the
Employee may ask the Company to treat the period of incapacity as sick leave and reclaim the annual leave.

**2.8.2**
To be able to claim Company sick pay, and reclaim the annual leave, the Employee must notify their Manager of their
incapacity immediately, and the usual requirements for medical evidence in this policy will also apply, even if the
Employee is abroad.

**2.8.3**
If the Employee is on sick leave, the Employee can choose to cancel any pre-arranged annual leave that otherwise
coincides with their sick leave.  The Employee should notify their manager as soon as possible that the Employee
wishes to do this.

**2.8.4**
If the Employee’s period of sick leave extends into the next holiday year, or if there is not enough time left in the
current holiday year to make it practicable to take their remaining holiday entitlement, the Employee can carry any
unused holiday entitlement over to the following leave year, to be used within three months of their return to work.
Any annual leave not taken within 18 months of the end of the holiday year in which it accrues (whether or not the
Employee has returned to work) will be lost.

work, and any of their work that requires attention.  Such contact is intended to provide reassurance and will be kept

## 2.10 Medical Examinations


**2.10.1**
The Company may, at any time in operating this policy, require the Employee to consent to a medical examination by
the Company’s Occupational Health Advisor and/or a Doctor nominated by the Company (at the Company’s expense).

**2.10.2**
The Employee will be asked to agree that any report produced in connection with any such examination may be
disclosed to the Company and that the Company may discuss the contents of the report with the Company’s advisors
and the relevant Doctor.

## 2.11

The Role of Occupational Health

**2.11.1**
The Company recognises and supports the role that Occupational Health can play in attendance management.  The
Company seeks to develop close liaison with the Occupational Health Services who can provide Management with
clear advice and guidance on individual cases that may be referred.
2.11.2  Occupational Health offer expert medical guidance, welfare assistance and objective support to Employees with health
problems.  For example: -
- Whether or not there is a significant medical reason to account for the absence problems;
- Assessing individual Employees with ill health problems, by recommending treatment, liaising with GPs or
specialists.  Please note they cannot, and will not prescribe medication or authorise treatment;
- Advising Human Resources on ill health terminations;
- Undertaking specific investigations to determine whether there are any environmental factors underlying
particular sickness absence problems;
- Structuring a return to work programme in conjunction with Human Resources;
- Providing medical support for the Company’s decision to terminate due to ill health.

**2.11.3**
In all cases, Occupational Health will be recorded and placed in the Employee’s Personnel File.  The Company will keep
all records in accordance with the Data Protection Act 1998 (as amended).

## 2.12 Measuring Absence


**2.12.1**
The Company has chosen to use Bradford Factors as a method of managing frequent short term absence.  Bradford
Factors identify persistent short term absence by considering the number of spells of absence, as well as their duration.

**2.12.2**
Bradford Factors are calculated using rolling 52 weeks’ attendance data under the following formula.

A x D – Where A is the number of individual absences and D is the number of days of total absence.

For example:

10 x one day absences: 10 x 10 x 10 = 1000

1 x ten-day absence: 1 x 1 x 10 = 10

## 2.13 Return-To-Work Interviews


**2.13.1**
If the Employee has been absent on sick leave, the Company will arrange for the Employee to have a Return-To-Work
Interview with their Line Manager.  This is usually within 48 hours of a return to work.

**2.13.2**
A Return-To-Work Interview enables the Company to confirm the details of the Employee’s absence.  It also gives
the Employee the opportunity to raise any concerns or questions the Employee may have, and to bring any relevant
matters to the Company’s attention. The Employee should advise their Line of any medication or treatment that they
are receiving.

**2.13.3**
Where a Doctor has provided a certificate stating the Employee “may be fit for work” the Company will usually hold
a return-to work interview to discuss any additional measures that may be needed to facilitate their return to work,
taking account of their Doctor’s advice.


## 2.14 Returning to Work from a Long-Term Sick Absence


**2.14.1**
The Company is committed to helping Employees return to work from long-term sickness absence.  As part of the
Company’s sickness absence meetings procedure (See section 2.15) the Company will, where appropriate, and
possible, support return to work by: -
- Obtaining medical advice;
- Making reasonable adjustments to the workplace, working practices and working hours;
- Considering redeployment and/or
- Agreeing a return to work programme with everyone affected.

**2.14.2**
If the Employee is unable to return to work in the longer term, the Company will consider whether the Employee is
entitled to any benefits under their contract and/or any insurance schemes the Company operates.

## 2.15 Sickness Absence Meetings Procedure


**2.15.1**
The Company may apply this procedure whenever the Company consider it necessary, including, for example,

if the Employee: -
- Has been absent due to illness on a number of occasions and has reached the trigger levels in section 2.16;
- Has discussed matters at a return-to-work interview that require investigation;
- Has been absent for more than four weeks and/or
- Has otherwise had absence from work which causes concern for the Company and requires discussion and
consideration at a formal meeting.

**2.15.2**
Unless it is impractical to do so, the Company will give the Employee at least two days’ written notice of the date, time
and place of a sickness absence meeting.  The Company will put any concerns about their sickness absence and the
basis for those concerns in writing or otherwise why the meeting is being called.  A reasonable opportunity for the
Employee to consider this information before a meeting will be provided.

**2.15.3**
The meeting will usually be conducted by the Employee’s Line Manager and will be attended by a member of the
Human Resources Department.  The Employee may bring a companion with the Employee to the meeting

(See section 2.17).

**2.15.4**
The Employee must take all reasonable steps to attend a meeting.  Failure to do so, without good reason, may be
treated as misconduct.  If the Employee or their companion are unable to attend at the time specified, the Employee
should immediately inform their Line Manager and/or Human Resources who will seek to agree an alternative time.

**2.15.5**
A meeting may be adjourned if the Company is awaiting receipt of information, needs to gather any further information
or give consideration to matters discussed at a previous meeting.  The Employee will be given a reasonable opportunity
to consider any new information obtained before the meeting is reconvened.

**2.15.6**
Confirmation of any decision made at a meeting, the reasons for it, and of the right to appeal will be given to the
Employee as soon as practicable after a sickness absence meeting, and within 10 days.

**2.15.7**
Any decision to terminate under this procedure will normally be with full notice or payment in lieu of notice.

**2.15.8**
If, at the time, the Line Manager or Human Resources considers that the Employee has taken or is taking sickness
absence when the Employee is not unwell, they may refer matters to be dealt with under the Company’s Disciplinary
Procedure.

## 2.16 Frequent Short Term Absences


**2.16.1**
If an Employee has a poor attendance record, there will come a time when the inconvenience caused by the length
and/or frequency of the absences means that some action must be taken.

**2.16.2**
It may even be necessary to dismiss an Employee in these circumstances, after appropriate warnings, and all reasonable
support and assistance have been given.  This applies even if the absence is for genuine sickness, covered by a Doctor’s
Statement of Fitness for Work (a “Fit Note”).

**2.16.3**
In all cases, the Company will take into account:
- The nature of the illness or illnesses
- The likelihood of recurrence or some other illness arising


- The length of the various absences
- The periods of good health between them
- The need for the Company to have the work done
- Any steps that the Company can reasonably take to help the Employee improve their attendance record,
including any suggestions made by their Doctor on a Fit Note
- The impact of the Employee’s absences on their fellow Employees.

**2.16.4**
In the event that the Employee’s attendance record is unacceptable, the procedure set out at 2.15 will be followed.
All formal meetings held under this procedure will follow the procedure set out in sections 2.15 and 2.17 on the
arrangements for and the right to be accompanied at sickness absence meetings.

**2.16.5**
A full investigation of the facts will be carried out by the Employee’s Line Manager who will review their attendance
record and the reasons for their absences.

**2.16.6**
The Employee’s Line Manager may seek medical evidence where it would assist the Company in determining: -
- The cause of the absences
- The reasons for their frequency
- The likelihood of them continuing in the future
- Any reasonable steps that the Company could take to help the Employee improve their attendance record.

**2.16.7 Trigger Levels**


The Company will refer to the Bradford Factor report for instances of frequent short term absences.

**2.16.9**
Where there is no evidence of an underlying medical reason for the frequent short term absences, the Company will
apply the following trigger points: -

100 points and 3 occurrences will usually result in a Level One Warning. This is given at the Return to Work Interview
(Section 2.13) by the Line Manager, and then confirmed in writing to the Employee by Human Resources.

200 points and 4 occurrences – If the Employee fails to achieve the standards set out at level one, then a formal
meeting may be held to review attendance.  The Employee will be required to provide an explanation for their absence
and to look for ways they can reduce their absence.  The Employee may be issued with a Level Two Written Warning.

300 points and 4 occurrences – If the Employee fails to achieve the standards set out at level two, then a formal
meeting may be held to review attendance.  The Employee will be required to provide an explanation for their absence
and to look for ways they can reduce their absence.  The Employee may be issued with a Level Three Final Written
Warning.  The Employee will be informed: -
- That their level of absenteeism is unacceptable and must improve;
- Of the steps, if any, the Company will take to help them reduce that level to an acceptable level;
- How and by what time their attendance level must improve, and
- What the consequences will be if it does not do so.

400 points – If the Employee fails to achieve the standards set out at level three, then a formal meeting may be held
to review attendance.  The Employee will be required to provide an explanation for their absence and to look for ways
they can reduce their absence.  At this stage, if the Company is satisfied that: -
- the Employee’s attendance is unlikely to improve within a reasonable period;
- that there is nothing further that the Company can reasonably do to help the Employee improve and maintain
a satisfactory attendance record;
- the Employee has unreasonably refused to co-operate in the trial of any adjustments that have been reasonably
proposed by the Company,


the Employee may be dismissed in accordance with this procedure on the basis that the Employee is no longer capable
of doing work of the kind that they were employed to do.
2.16.10 If there has been a substantial improvement in the Employee’s attendance, but the required standard has not yet been
reached and/or maintained, the Company may extend or re-issue the Final Warning.
2.16.11 In exceptional circumstances, one or both of the warning stages may be omitted, depending on the Employee’s
absence record, its impact on the business and the Employee’s response to any earlier informal warnings.


2.16.12 Where there is evidence of an underlying medical reason for the frequent short term absences, the Company, at its
discretion, may not apply the above triggers, but will follow the rest of the principles set out at Section 2.16 and the
procedure at Section 2.15 when considering the Employee’s ongoing employment with the Company.

## 2.17 Right to be Accompanied at Meetings


**2.17.1**
The Employee may bring a work colleague companion or Trade Union Representative to any meeting or appeal meeting
under this procedure.

**2.17.2**
Employees are allowed reasonable time off from duties without loss of pay to act as a companion.  However, they are
not obliged to act as a companion and may decline a request if they so wish.

**2.17.3**
The Company may, at the Company’s discretion permit other companions (for example, a family member) where this
will help overcome particular difficulties caused by a disability, or difficulty understanding English.

**2.17.4**
The Employee’s chosen companion can act as a witness, address the hearing to put and sum up the Employee’s case
and respond on behalf of the Employee to any views expressed at the meeting, and confer with the Employee during
the hearing.  The companion does not have the right to answer questions on the Employee’s behalf, address the hearing
if the Employee does not wish it, or prevent the Employer from explaining their case.  It is the Employee’s responsibility
to ensure that their chosen companion is willing to act as a witness and is available to attend the meeting, with the
exception of where the Employee has been suspended, and in this case, Employees should request their witness
through HR.

## 2.18 Long Term Absence


**2.18.1**
This procedure is designed to deal with the unfortunate situation of a long absence through illness or injury.

**2.18.2**
At all stages, the Company’s approach will be governed by the Employee’s condition as understood by the Company.

**2.18.3**
The Employee may be asked to give their consent to the matter being referred to their GP, a GP nominated by the
Company, and/or another medical specialist (such as an Occupational Health specialist) in order that the Company may
consult him or her regarding the Employee’s medical position. The Company will carefully consider any medical report
received and seek further clarification if necessary.

**2.18.4**
If the Employee unreasonably refuses to give consent in such circumstances, the Company will take whatever
action is appropriate on the basis of the medical evidence available to it.  A refusal to co-operate may also limit the
Company’s ability to consider any adjustments to the Employee’s working environment which may otherwise have
been appropriate.

**2.18.5**
It the Company chooses to obtain further information from the Employee’s GP, the Employee will be informed that
the Company is considering the Employee’s future continued employment and the Doctor will be told of the nature
of their job and duties.  The Doctor will be asked to provide a prognosis as to when the Employee is likely to return to
their duties.   If their illness or injury results in or from a disability, the Company will also ask the Doctor whether there
are any reasonable adjustments which could be made to the Employee’s duties or workstation which would facilitate
a return to work.

**2.18.6**
Once the medical position has been ascertained, either the Line Manager or a more Senior Manager will meet the
Employee to discuss the situation with them.  Human Resources will also be present at this meeting.
2.18.7  During any discussions consideration will be given to the Company’s need for the work to be done and the Employee’s
need for time to recover their health.  The Company will also take into account the nature of the illness, the likely
length of the continuing absence, the prospects of the Employee resuming work and the particular circumstances of
the case.

**2.18.8**
If the Employee’s illness or injury results from or in a disability, the Company will also consider whether there are any
reasonable adjustments that the Company can make which would facilitate the Employee’s return to work.

**2.18.9**
In all ill health cases the Company will consider whether there is any alternative employment in the business where
a return to the original job is impossible or where it is not reasonably practicable to make adjustments which would
facilitate a return.


2.18.10 The Company reserves the right to terminate the contract of any Employee absent through sickness or injury at any
time.  The timing of any decision to dismiss will largely depend on: -
- the nature of the illness;
- the likely date of return;
- the nature of the Employee’s job and the needs of the business;
- how integral the Employee’s contribution is to the running and income of the business;
- the disruptive influence of an absent Employee on other Employees;
- the impact on the absent Employee who may be disadvantaged in the work place, and who may require
retraining.

The company will also take into consideration:
- any adjustments that have been tried, but proved to be unsuccessful, where there are no other adjustments
that might be made;
- whether the Employee has unreasonably refused to co-operate in the trial of any adjustments that have been
reasonably proposed by the Company.


## 2.19 Ill Health and Capability


**2.19.1**
Following an absence from work due to ill health, in the event an employee returns to work, but is unable to carry out
the full requirements of their role, the Company will arrange to meet with the Employee in a formal capacity to discuss
this.

**2.19.2**
Full consideration will be given to an alternative role in the business, but where this is not possible the Company may
consider dismissing the employee on the grounds of their ill health and capability to return to their full duties.

**2.19.3**
Employees will be invited to a formal meeting to discuss alternative employment, and any other alternatives to possible
termination of their contract of employment.  Employees have the right to be accompanied at this meeting as detailed
in Section 2.17.

**2.19.4**
The purpose of the meeting will be to jointly discuss the following:
- Evidence of current medical condition and documentation to support;
- Current Occupational Health Report;
- Likelihood of returning to work in your  full role (or in an alternative role);
- Any work place adjustments, including adjustments that have previously been tried, but proved to be
unsuccessful, where there are no other adjustments that might be made;
- Whether the Employee has unreasonably refused to co-operate in the trial of any adjustments that have been
reasonably proposed by the Company;
- Ill health retirement on the grounds of capability.

**2.19.5**
In the event of dismissal on the grounds of ill health capability, the Employee will be entitled to be paid in lieu in
contractual notice, as detailed in the Employee’s Terms and Conditions of Employment.  The Employee will not be
required to work this notice period.

## 2.20 Appeals


**2.20.1**
The Employee may appeal against the outcome of any formal meeting under this procedure and the Employee may
bring a companion to an Appeal Meeting (See section 2.17).

**2.20.2**
An appeal should be made in writing, stating the full grounds of the appeal, to the Company Secretary, or HR Director,
within five days of the date on which the decision was sent to the Employee.  An appeal should be on one of the
following grounds: -
- The sanction imposed is too severe and disproportionate;
- There has been a procedural irregularity, in which case details of the alleged irregularity should be given;
- Further facts/information not available or considered at the time have emerged which require consideration.
In these cases, full details should be given.

**2.20.3**
The Employee will be given written notice of an appeal meeting, and the details of the Manager who the Company
Secretary or HR Director has nominated to chair the Appeal Hearing.  In cases of dismissal the appeal will be held as
soon as possible.  Any new matters raised in an appeal may delay an appeal meeting if further investigation is required.



**2.20.4**
The Employee will be provided with written details of any new information which comes to light before an appeal
meeting.  The Employee will also be given a reasonable opportunity to consider this information before the meeting.
2.20.5   Where practicable, an appeal meeting will be conducted by a Manager senior to the individual who conducted the
sickness absence meeting.

**2.20.6**
Depending on the circumstances, an appeal meeting may be a complete rehearing of the matter or a  of the original
decision.
2.20.7  The final decision will be confirmed in writing, within 10 days of the appeal meeting.  There will be no further right of
appeal.

**2.20.8**
The date that the dismissal takes effect will not be delayed pending the outcome of an appeal.  However, if the appeal
is successful, the decision to dismiss will be revoked with no loss of continuity or pay.
