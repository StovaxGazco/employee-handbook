# Section 46: Uniform Dress Code


## 46.1 Purpose and Scope


**46.1.1** The purpose of this Policy is to set out the expectations of the Company in relation to Corporate Dress Code and the wearing of Company Uniforms. The Dress Code is necessary in order to convey a professional image of the Company and its Employees.


**46.1.2** The Company considers the way Employees dress and their appearance to be of importance in portraying a

professional image.

## 46.2 Policy Principles


**46.2.1** The Policy applies to all staff including those with temporary contracts, Agency Workers and Students on work experience that are working at the Company.

**46.2.2** The Uniform and Dress Code Policy is designed to guide Managers and Employees on the standards of dress and appearance that the Company expects. All Employees’ appearance must be professional at all times within the work place and when representing the Company at outside meetings and events.

**46.2.3** The Policy is not exhaustive in defining acceptable and unacceptable standards of dress and appearance, and Employees must use common sense in adhering to the principles underpinning the policy.

## 46.3 Responsibilities


**46.3.1** Employees

**46.3.1.1** All Employees are individually responsible for their general presentation, appearance and personal hygiene, and have a responsibility to consider how others may perceive their appearance. Employees are responsible for following the standards of uniform and dress and appearance laid down in this Policy and must understand how the Dress Code relates to their working environment, health and safety, their specific role and duties, and their contact with others during their employment.

**46.3.2** Managers

**46.3.2.1** Managers are responsible for ensuring that the Policy and Dress Code is adhered to at all times in respect of the Employees they manage. Managers must also ensure that all new Employees are aware of required standards of dress code during any staff induction.

**46.3.3** Failure to adhere to the Company’s standard of dress and appearance may constitute misconduct and could result in formal disciplinary procedures being implemented.

## 46.4

Company Dress Code

**46.4.1** Employees supplied with a Corporate Uniform

**46.4.1.1** Within the Stovax Group, designated Employee groups are required to wear Corporate Uniforms provided by the Company. Where the Company issues uniforms, Employees must take responsibility to ensure that good care is taken of the items provided and they must always be worn in a clean and presentable fashion.

**46.4.2** Employees not issued with a Corporate Uniform

**46.4.2.1** Employees are expected to dress appropriately in accordance with the duties of their post.

**46.4.2.2** For Employees not required to wear corporate uniforms, examples of acceptable staff clothing include a

combination of:

Female Employees

Skirts, shirts, smart tops, smart jumpers, jackets, dresses, suits, smart trousers, smart shoes.


Males Employees

Business suits, smart trousers, jackets, smart jumpers, long or short sleeved shirts with collars, ties, smart

enclosed shoes.

## 46.5 Unacceptable Clothing


**46.5.1** For Employees who are not provided with a Corporate Uniform, the following items are examples of unacceptable clothing either on the grounds of health and safety or for the Company’s public image:
- Transparent or see-through tops, skirts, dresses or shirts;
- Denim Jeans (black jeans are acceptable if they are not washed out)
- Clothing with tears, holes or rips;
- Low cut t-shirts or tops;
- Crop tops or any item of clothing that exposes midriffs;
- Combat trousers/cargo pants;
- Sportswear, e.g. tracksuits, vest tops, shorts, rugby or football shirts;
- Baseball caps and hats;
- Clothing with slogans or graphics which may cause offence;
- Flip-flops (leather or otherwise)

**46.5.2** Employees are encouraged to adopt a common-sense approach with regard to clothing, jewellery or glasses which they wear to work. Employees must ensure that articles are appropriate to the type of work they carry out and also be mindful of any potential damage or loss to their personal property, which may arise as a result of carrying out

their duties.

**46.5.3** Employees are not permitted to wear jewellery in all Factory or Warehouse areas for health and safety reasons including bracelets, earrings (excluding studs if an Employee has pierced ears), necklaces, rings or other jewellery. Wedding rings may be worn but must be covered with a plaster whilst working with any potentially dangerous equipment.

**46.5.4** Safety clothing and Personal Protective Equipment (PPE) is an essential requirement in some areas and must be worn if an Employee is working in one of these areas. Examples of PPE include safety boots, eye protection, gloves, hearing protection, hard hats and high visibility work wear.

**46.5.5** If an Employee is not dressed appropriately, they will be asked to go home and change.  This time must be made up in agreement with the Employee’s Line Manager or will be taken unpaid.
