# Section 35: Probationary Period


## 35.1 Introduction


**35.1.1** The Stovax Group is committed to recruiting and appointing candidates with the necessary skills and experience to be able to carry out and be successful in the posts it advertises.

**35.1.2** Key to this process is effective planning in relation to defining the role and its requirements via a job description and identifying and selecting appropriate candidates through interviews and other selection techniques.

**35.1.3** However, it is also important to monitor and review the progress of Employees after starting to ensure that they receive the necessary training and support to be able to achieve their potential, and to address issues of poor performance or incapability at the earliest stage.

## 35.2 Purpose and Scope


**35.2.1** This procedure will apply to all newly appointed or promoted staff, including temporary staff (i.e. those on fixed term contracts, but not usually to those employed through an external agency).

**35.2.2** This policy applies to all members of staff.

## 35.3 Principles


**35.3.1** New and newly promoted members of staff can seek, and expect additional help in developing the skills and knowledge required to undertake their role and to meet the requirements as defined in their Job Description. It is intended to be a supportive and developmental process and the Company is committed to providing whatever assistance is reasonable to ensure that the probation period is successfully completed. However, should an acceptable standard of performance not be achieved, the Company reserves the right not to confirm the appointee in post and take whatever further action is required.

**35.3.2** During the probationary period there is a responsibility:-
- On the Line Manager, on behalf of the Company, to train and guide the probationer appropriately in order to fulfil the role they have been appointed to.  This is an on-going responsibility but is specifically monitored during the probationary period;
- On the newly appointed member of staff to demonstrate their capability to work to acceptable standards without requiring an unduly high level of supervision.

**35.4.1** The duration of the probationary periods may be varied by a Director taking account of the level and responsibilities of the post. Full details regarding the duration of probation periods are contained in individual Employee’s Contracts of Employment.

**35.4.2** Reviews must be carried out before the end of the probationary period.  The outcome of such review will be communicated in writing within five working days of the Probationary Review.

## 35.5 Continuous Assessment


**35.5.1** Assessment of performance and active support and development should be an ongoing process throughout the probationary period (and beyond) and not just undertaken at the formal review stages.  Informal probationary meetings may take place at any time to discuss any aspect of performance both below and above the standards expected against the requirements as defined in the Employee’s Job Description.


## 35.6 Criteria for Assessment


**35.6.1** The Probationer’s work record as a whole will be considered. Particular attention will be given to the patterns of improvement or deterioration that may give an indication for the future.

**35.6.2** Three key areas of review will be considered by the Line Manager:
- Performance – the ability of the Probationer to carry out the tasks and responsibilities of the post, their contribution to the service or team
- Conduct – i.e. how the Probationer conducts themselves, for example their work relationships with colleagues or any external individuals, especially customers or suppliers, general attitude to work and commitment to the role
- Attendance Record – time keeping, levels of sickness, absence etc.

**35.6.3** Note that where an individual has a poor attendance record directly related to sickness absence, the reasons for this will be carefully considered. Serious problems will be dealt with immediately in accordance with the [Absence Management Policy and Procedure](02-absence-management.md).

**35.6.4** These key areas are dealt with in the Probation Report Form (see Appendix 35.1) but are broken down to more specific, measurable assessment criteria. This Probation Report Form will be completed by the Line Manager.

## 35.7 Formal Review


**35.7.1** Human Resources will forward a reminder including the Probation Report Form (Appendix 35.1) to the Manager, not later than 2 weeks prior to the Probation Review due date.

**35.7.2** The Line Manager, in conjunction with HR, will be responsible for arranging the probation review meeting in accordance with clause 35.7.1 above.

**35.7.3** Where an Employee is either not to be confirmed in post, or there is an extension to their probationary period, HR will be in attendance, to take notes of the meeting, and to provide support and guidance.

**35.7.4** The Probationer will have the right to be accompanied to (but not represented by) the Hearing by a fellow Employee or Trade Union Representative.

**35.7.5** In addition to the review at the end of the probationary period, a formal review may also take place at any time during the probationary period (whether previously extended or not) where it has been identified that: -
- The Probationer’s work performance is unsatisfactory; or;
- That there has been no improvement in poor performance; or;
- Where there is a record of poor attendance.

**35.7.6** During the meeting a Probation Report (see Appendix 35.1) should be completed by the Line Manager and where relevant in conjunction with a representative from Human Resources. At the end of the report the Line Manager will need to make one of four recommendations: -
- **a)** If the Performance of the Probationer is satisfactory the Employee will be confirmed in post.  A letter will be sent to the Employee from Human Resources on receipt of the completed and signed Probation Form confirming the Employee in post.
- **b)** If the Line Manager considers that the Probationer may be unsuitable for permanent employment, or there has been insufficient improvement in performance following additional training and support, the Line Manager, in consultation with Human Resources if appropriate, should decide whether either: -
- additional time is required to be able to fully assess the suitability of the Probationer.  In these circumstances an extension to the Probationary Period will be agreed (see below). The outcome of the review will be to highlight the areas where improvement is required and to suggest methods and timescales for achieving them. The Probationer will be informed that failure to reach the expected standards by the specified date may mean they will not be confirmed in post.


- a further review is unlikely to lead to any substantial improvement and the decision should be taken not to confirm the Probationer in post. A decision not to confirm in post will be taken by the Line Manager in consultation with the Human Resources Manager and after reference to a Director. Those whose employment is ended by the Company within their Probation Period are deemed to be ‘non-confirmed in post’.
- **c)** Where an Employee has been promoted, but has failed to meet the standard of work or conduct expected of the promoted position, then the decision may be made to return the Employee to the position they were in before they were promoted or an alternative position of a similar standing to the position they were in before they were promoted if possible.

In all cases, a copy of the report should be given to the Probationer for them to sign and return to the Line Manager.
The Probationer may, if they wish, keep a copy of the report for their own records. The original should then be
forwarded to the Human Resources Department for review.

## 35.8 Extensions to Probationary Periods


**35.8.1** Extensions of Probation Periods must not be used as a means of putting off a difficult decision and are only justified in certain circumstances, for example: -
- **a)** Where a Probationer has not yet been given sufficient training to do the job to the required standard; or
- **b)** Where a one-off bout of sickness has led to a long period of absence, and the extension is necessary in aiding the Manager to reach a reasonable decision; or
- **c)** Where a Probationer has made some progress and given indications that they can meet the required standard but not yet done so on a consistent basis.

**35.8.2** The decision to extend a Probationary Period must be made after consultation with Human Resources or a Director. In the case of an extension the Probationer should be informed, in writing, of the reasons for the terms of the extension. A period of three months should be sufficient time for a decision to be made. The Probationer has a right to appeal against the extension of a Probationary Period as set out in Section 35.11.

**35.8.3** Action at the end of an extended Probationary Period:
- **a)** If, at the end of an extended Probationary Period the member of staff has achieved an acceptable standard of performance they will be confirmed in post.
- **b)** If, at the end of or during an extended Probationary Period, the member of staff has failed to achieve an acceptable standard of performance and it is not considered likely to improve, then a formal Probationary Hearing will be held (see Section 35.7).

## 35.9 Appeals


**35.9.1** Where the decision of the Line Manager is to extend the Probationary Period the Employee will have the right to appeal to the Company Secretary or HR Director. The appeal will be chaired by the Company Secretary or a nominated Director or Senior Manager.

**35.9.2** Appeals should be made, in writing, to the Company Secretary or HR Director. The appeal should be made within 5 days of the original hearing and should state the grounds on which the appeal is based. This period may be extended at the absolute discretion of the Company Secretary or HR Director whose decision will be final.

**35.9.3** An appeal should be on one of the following grounds: -
- **a)** There has been a procedural irregularity, in which case details of the alleged irregularity should be given;
- **b)** Further facts/information not available or considered at the time have emerged which require consideration. In these cases, full details should be given.

**35.9.4** Having received the grounds of appeal, the Company Secretary or HR Director will, where relevant, ensure that the appeal is not as a consequence of misunderstanding of the procedure to date.  If this appears to be the case an initial informal discussion may be undertaken to clarify matters.  In these cases, only if this proves unsuccessful will a formal appeal hearing be convened.

**35.9.5** The decision of the Company Secretary or nominated Senior Manager will be final.


## 35.10 Decisions not to Confirm in Post


**35.10.1** If a decision is taken not to confirm the Probationer in post they will have a right of appeal to the Company Secretary. The Appeal will be chaired by the Company Secretary, a Director or a Senior Manager nominated by the Company Secretary or HR Director.

**35.10.2** Appeals should be made in writing, within 5 working days of receipt of the written confirmation of the dismissal and addressed to the Company Secretary or HR Director. (This period may be extended at the absolute discretion of the Company Secretary or HR Director whose decision will be final).

**35.10.3** The grounds of appeal must be stated and should be encompassed by one of the following: -
- The employee considers the sanction applied is disproportionate to the reasons for the dismissal given in the Probationary Hearing.
- There has been a procedural irregularity in the Probationary Hearing
- There are new facts which have come to light which have a significant bearing on the proceeding.

**35.10.4** Having received the grounds of the appeal, the Company Secretary or HR Director, will, where relevant, ensure that the appeal is not as a consequence of a misunderstanding of the procedure to date.  If this appears to be the case an initial informal discussion may be undertaken to clarify matters.  In these cases, only if this proves unsuccessful will a formal appeal be convened.

**35.10.5** The appeal will, where practicable, be held within ten working days of receiving the Employee’s ground for appeal.

**35.10.6** The appeal hearing will take place at the Company premises, and the Employee will have the opportunity to explain their grounds of appeal.  The Employee will be informed at least two full working days in advance of the date, time and location of the hearing and must take all reasonable steps to attend.  The appeal hearing will usually be conducted by the Company Secretary or an appointed Director or Senior Manager.  The form of the appeal hearing will be at the discretion of the Company Secretary, Director or Manager hearing the appeal.  An appeal will usually involve a review of all the relevant documentation including the Employee’s grounds for appeal.

**35.10.7** The appeal process will not normally be an opportunity to repeat the Probationary Hearing.  A repeat of a Hearing will only be allowed where an Employee can show that significant new evidence has come to light which could have had an impact on the decision of the Probationary Panel.

**35.10.8** The Employee may be accompanied to the appeal hearing by another Employee of the Company or Trade Union Representative if they wish.  If the Employee’s chosen companion is unavailable on the proposed date of the appeal this, and this is notified to the Company in advance, the hearing may be postponed by up to five working days at the Employee’s request and subject to the Employee proposing an alternative time and ate within five working days of the original date.

**35.10.9** Should the Employee be unable to attend the appeal hearing, due to circumstances beyond their control and unforeseeable at the time the Appeal Hearing was arranged, the Company will seek to re-arrange a mutually acceptable date.  The rescheduled appeal hearing may proceed and a decision may be taken in the Employee’s absence, if the Employee fails to attend the rescheduled appeal hearing without good reason.

**35.10.10** The Employee’s chosen companion can act as a witness, address the hearing to put and sum up the Employee’s case and respond on behalf of the Employee to any views expressed at the meeting, and confer with the Employee during the hearing.  The companion does not have the right to answer questions on the Employee’s behalf, address the hearing if the Employee does not wish it, or prevent the Employer from explaining their case.  It is the Employee’s responsibility to ensure that their chosen companion is willing to act as a witness and is available to attend the meeting, with the exception of where the Employee has been suspended, and in this case, Employees should request their witness through HR.

**35.10.11** Following the appeal hearing the decision of the Probationary Hearing will be reviewed, and can either be removed, reduced or confirmed.  The outcome of the appeal will be relayed in writing within five working days of the appeal hearing being held.  The appeal decision will be final and cannot be further appealed.

**35.10.12** Until the outcome of the appeal has been decided, dismissal will remain in force.  If, following a successful appeal against dismissal, an Employee is reinstated, they will be reimbursed in respect of lost salary for the period between the dismissal and the date of reinstatement.


## 35.11 Notice Periods


**35.11.1** During the Probationary Period all Employees will be subject to an Employer’s one weeks’ notice period for termination of employment. After confirmation in post, the Employer’s notice period will be as set out in the Employee’s Personal Terms and Conditions of Employment.

**35.11.2** During the Probationary Period, newly promoted Employees will be subject to notice of dismissal in accordance with the Employee’s Personal Terms and Conditions of Employment or statutory provisions, whichever is the greater.

## 35.12 Revision or Termination of this Policy and Procedure


**35.12.1** This Policy and Procedure is not contractual and its operation will be periodically reviewed. Any amendment to it will be advised to staff who will also be informed of the date when the amendment will come into effect.
