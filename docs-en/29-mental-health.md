# Section 29: Mental Health & Wellbeing


## 29.1 Purpose and Scope

29.1.1  The Company has a duty of care requirement to look after the health and safety of employees, including their
wellbeing.  Mental wellbeing is relevant for all employees, which means every member of staff can play a part in
improving wellbeing in the workplace.  By addressing mental health issues, the company can improve the general
wellbeing of employees, reduce absenteeism and presenteeism, lower staff turnover, increase productivity, and help
promote the employment of those who have experienced mental health problems.

## 29.2 Aim of the Policy


**29.2.1**
The aim of the policy is to create a workplace culture that promotes and supports the health and  wellbeing of all
Employees.

## 29.3 Management and Leadership

29.3.1  The Company will equip managers and leaders with the skills to identify and assist those with mental ill health by:
- Appointing trained Mental Health First Aiders in key areas of the business, including HR
- Providing and raising awareness of mental health issues
- Promoting policies and actions that support mental wellbeing in the workplace
- Creating a culture that supports the wellbeing of Employees

## 29.4 Support for Employees


**29.4.1**
The Company has a designated Employee Assistance Programme via BUPA Healthcare which is available to all
employees https://cbt.bupa.com/signup/.   This service is entirely confidential and is also available to partners and
other dependants over the age of 16.  Leaflets and posters are visible throughout the Company detailing how to
access this service. The details are also available via Public Folders.

**29.4.2**
The company will take the following actions:
- Deliver non-judgemental support to any Employee experiencing a mental health issue
- Give all Employees access to the Mental Health Policy
- Deliver a thorough induction for all new starters, providing an outline of the organisation, the policies and the
roles they are expected to play
- Set realistic targets and deadlines for Employees to prevent excessively long working hours
- Deal with any conflict quickly and make sure the workplace is free from bullying, harassment, racism or
discrimination
- Ensure all Employees have clear Job Descriptions, objectives and responsibilities, as well as the training to do
their job well
- Ensure good communication between managers, Employees and teams.
- Encourage Employees to talk to a mental health first aider, HR, Occupational Health, a counsellor or GP
- If an Employee has been on long term sickness absence, ensure a gradual return to work with support at each
stage
- Treat all matters relating to Employee mental ill health in the strictest confidence.

## 29.5 Other Actions


**29.5.1**
Physical activity keeps the body strong and health, and can improve mental health by decreasing symptoms of
depression, anxiety, pain and loneliness.  Physical activity can also improve focus, performance, sleep and energy
levels.

**29.5.2**
The Company strongly recommends that Employees to take a lunch-break during the working day, away from their
work stations and encourages them to consider going for a short walk.   A short burst of 10 minutes brisk walking
increases mental alertness, energy and a creates a more positive mood.

**29.5.3**
The Company also has a ‘[Cycle to Work Scheme](10-cycle-to-work.md)’ and encourages eligible Employees to consider participating in this
scheme. Further details can be found in the Employee Handbook, section 10.


## 29.6 [Health & Safety](22-health-safety.md)


**29.6.1**
The company recognises that workplace stress is a health and safety issue and will:-
- Identify workplace stress/factors/scenarios and carry out risk assessments of the business
- Provide training in good management practices
- Provide resources to help managers implement the company’s workplace mental health and wellbeing policy
- Offer support through qualified mental health first aiders.

## 29.7 Reviewing and Monitoring


**29.7.1**
The Group HR Director will be responsible for reviewing the workplace health and wellbeing policy, as well as
monitoring its effectiveness.  The policy’s effectiveness can be measured through:
- Feedback from Employees
- Annual Reviews/Appraisals
- Staff sickness, presenteeism and staff turnover levels
- Exit interviews
- Use of Occupational Health
- Staff complaints or referrals
- Feedback from the Company’s Mental Health First Aiders
